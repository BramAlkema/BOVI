# The Cast — contracts named after their popularisers

Each implemented contract carries the name of the thinker whose mechanism it attempts to execute. The suite _is_ the lineage, instantiated—but the audit also found that not every name deserves independent state. Some ideas are views, extensions, or factories around another room. Read top-to-bottom and you get the intellectual history of money as a buildable cascade, resting stone by stone on `docs/FOUNDATIONS.md`.

**V1 is now treated as the reference baseline, not the V2 architecture; Phase 0 still has to freeze and tag it.** The repository now contains twenty-one Solidity source contracts and one hundred twenty-two passing Foundry tests across nine suites, but a green demonstrator is not an institutional closure or security result. The ground-up redesign, including the disposition of every contract, the distributed-keepership correction, the cold-start/continuation split, and the proof–simulation–deployment gates, is in [`V2-OVERHAUL-PLAN.md`](V2-OVERHAUL-PLAN.md). The normative selection slice and its implemented JSON-safe source, validator, deterministic ABM/structural replay, strict source-seed-horizon trace verification and evidence adapters are documented in [`docs/v2/SELECTION-LIFECYCLE.md`](../docs/v2/SELECTION-LIFECYCLE.md). `AcceptanceThreshold.sol` now supplies a narrower Solidity projection beside its preserved V1 threshold trace. `BilateralModeAgreement.sol`, Kocherlakota's isolated V2 posting state, and `SettlementEpisode.sol` add a second narrow projection in which bilateral mode, posting, tender, residual and discharge acknowledgement remain distinct. `ReputationMemory.sol` now adds an isolated V1 contextual-memory path beside its preserved global-score trace. `ContestableAssertion.sol` preserves `ChallengeBond.sol` as the V1 compatibility trace while adding neutral outcomes, paid resolver/fallback clocks, inconclusive refunds and one-time consumer use. `ObservationAggregation.sol` preserves `SharedNumeraire.sol` while adding typed, bounded, challengeable and stale-halting evidence epochs with no downstream call. `BilateralTermsSchedule.sol` preserves `IndexedObligation.sol` while adding bilateral terms, independent due periods, mutually accepted index observations, acknowledged partial performance, explicit forgiveness, bounded cure and bilateral termination without a ledger or token call. None is a complete V2 derivation, proof target or production deployment. Thinker names remain lineage in V2; they no longer determine module boundaries.

**We join a conversation, we do not found one.** A literature scan (June 2026) confirmed every part of the framework is borrowed and well-homed; the cast below is the honour roll. Where a thinker gives an _executable mechanism_ they get a contract or protocol blueprint; where they give the _value theory or the anthropology_ they are honoured as a **patron-spirit** (see Notes), because you cannot instantiate a premise. The near-programmable cards, including state, calls, transitions, invariants, and trust boundaries, live in [`docs/book/appendix-executable-canon.md`](../docs/book/appendix-executable-canon.md).

## The six stones, cast

So the cascade visibly rests on `FOUNDATIONS.md` — which thinker carries which stone:

- **Stone 1 — the pull** (money pulled by the need for a memory of who-gave-and-didn't-receive): the patron-spirits, no single room — _Menger, Simmel, Graeber_.
- **Stone 2 — the ledger / credit**: `SignedPositionLedger` (memory) + `MitchellInnes` (credit) + `Mehrling` (balance-sheet hierarchy); its V2 postings feed selection only as narrow ledger evidence.
- **Stone 3 — floorless-but-fruitful value**: patron-spirits _Simmel + Patinkin_ (premise, not a room); `SelectionLifecycle` remains the source authority for selection-vs-worth, priming-vs-continuation and coexistence. `AcceptanceThreshold` preserves its compatibility trace and now projects a bounded subset into Solidity without treating the legacy score as lifecycle evidence.
- **Stone 4 — the axioms** (resolution + integrity; the skims live in the sufficiency-gaps): `PriceDiscoveryCheck` (known-terms gap — the one genuinely information-driven skim), contextual `ReputationMemory` memory plus counterparty-owned future responses (the credit-form performance residual), `SharedNumeraire` (the unit/known-ness), `ExperimentCalibrationEvidence` (the experimental test of the enforcement row: the token's floor at zero _is_ possession-is-settlement, and lifting it is what makes continued performance a separate institutional question). _`NominalExposureMeter` was listed here as the known-supply gap and has been moved off this axis (audit, 2026-08): his losers are fixed-lease and fixed-wage holders, not the ill-informed, and QE was pre-announced and redistributed anyway. It now meters exposure on the **unit-of-account / nominal-contract** axis, where `IndexedObligation` is the defence and it is the meter._
- **Stone 5 — the bolt-ons / unbundling**: `Brunnermeier`, `Lietaer`, `Baumol`, `IndexedObligation` (store/unit/stability sent off the rail to their own tools).
- **Stone 6 — the melt** (medium ≠ store): Gesell overlay, `Lietaer`, `CountercyclicalElasticity`, `IndexedObligation`.
- **The institutional layer** (extraction is selected by a power contest): `Galbraith`, `GovernedDials`, `Minsky`, `Keynes`, and the modes — `ModePermissionGate` + `Bohannan` + `Zelizer`.

## Tier 2 — extensions (demonstrations of reach)

The cast above is the building. These are **not stones**: the framework's sufficiency argument does not rest on any of them, and removing the whole tier leaves `FOUNDATIONS.md` untouched. They exist to answer one objection — _this is isolated stuff_ — by showing the architecture explaining outcomes outside itself.

**Admission rule.** An entry must show a **monetary parameter driving a non-monetary outcome**, via a published result, isolable to one mechanism. Three of the four most obvious candidates fail it (see the rejects below); a rule that admits everything we like is a preference, not a rule.

Precedent for the tier's _shape_: `ModePermissionGate` is already a boundary contract — it describes no ledger machinery, it guards where the record may not go, on the social side. Tier 2 guards the other edge: what the record's parameters do to things that are not records.

**Rejected, on the rule — the exclusions are the load-bearing part:**

- **Radford**, _The Economic Organisation of a P.O.W. Camp_ (_Economica_ 1945). Tier 1 **evidence** for a selection profile, not a lifecycle mechanism and not a monetary parameter driving a non-monetary outcome. Out of _this_ tier, not out of the canon.
- **Cagan** (1956), hyperinflation money demand. The outcome is money demand — still monetary. Readmit only if anchored to a real consequence: barter re-emergence, or the collapse of long contracts.
- **Austerity and mortality** (Stuckler & Basu). Fiscal, not monetary; identification contested. Out.
- **Exchange rates and deforestation.** Real depreciation → export agriculture → forest clearing. Tempting because it flatters an audience we would like to reach, which is precisely why it stays out until there is a citation worth defending. A lead, not an entry.
- **Nordhaus / DICE.** An integrated assessment model, not an isolable mechanism. Out because the tier admits mechanisms, not whole model worlds.

## Finding the positions

`scripts/position-audit.py` walks the compiled AST and lists every place `msg.sender` is tested against a stored identity — the structural signature of an authority position.

```
forge build --force --ast && python3 scripts/position-audit.py
```

Current forced-build reading: **26 authority positions across 14 contracts, none of them a declared type.** Without `--force`, an unchanged artifact may lack an AST and the script silently skips it; that previously produced 24 across 13 and omitted `AppointedOffice`. A hand audit of the same cast found eight. That gap is the argument: `address` is one type for the payer, the steward, the reporter, the oracle and the person they act upon, so a position can exist everywhere and be counted nowhere. A missing field is a compile error; a missing category is silence.

The tool over-collects — it cannot tell an authority from a counterparty, so `IndexedObligation.payer` and `ChallengeBond.asserter` show up alongside `SignedPositionLedger.steward`. Structure alone cannot make that distinction; only a declaration can. Which is the other half of the work, and why the checker and the type are one deliverable rather than two.

## Running the suite

`forge-std` is a git submodule, so a fresh clone needs it fetched before anything builds:

```
git submodule update --init --recursive
forge test
```

One hundred twenty-two tests across nine files, including `test/CoreE2E.t.sol` (the legacy bench engine) and focused suites for the amended selection, posting/settlement, contextual memory, contest, observation and indexed-obligation paths. All twenty-one Solidity source contracts compile under solc 0.8.20. Five contracts currently have no executed test path: `ExperimentCalibrationEvidence`, `NominalExposureMeter`, `ChallengeBond`, `SinkCapacityEvidence`, and `PriceDiscoveryCheck`; passing tests remain a reproducible demonstrator baseline, not adequate V2 or security coverage.

The separate V2 selection simulator compiles and runs from the repository root:

```
npm run build:selection-lifecycle
npm run simulate:selection-lifecycle -- --list
npm run simulate:selection-lifecycle -- --scenario knights-tally-rope --seed lecture-demo
```

The last command writes the manifest, summary, structurally replayable event log and terminal state to `tmp/selection-lifecycle/knights-tally-rope.json`. Structural replay checks present-event ordering and guards; imported deterministic logs require the exported `verifyDeterministicTrace` with a trusted scenario, seed and tick horizon, which reruns the model and compares every event payload. Use `--help` for tick overrides, output paths and repeated safe-integer JSON-Pointer sweep axes. Repair, exit and succession still require accepted external findings, and no V2 proof or deployable SelectionLifecycle Solidity contract is present.

Worth saying once, because it cost two parse errors to learn: **a contract that has been read carefully and never compiled is prose.** `NominalExposureMeter` and `ExperimentCalibrationEvidence` were written, reviewed, cross-referenced in `NOTATION.md` and cited in the book before either was put through a compiler, and both were broken in ways one build would have caught. Build before you document.

## Notes

- **Patron-spirits — honoured by the whole building, not a single room.** A premise cannot be instantiated, so the framework's deepest debts are named here rather than coded:
  - _value theory_ — **Menger** (subjective/marginal value, the building completes it), **Simmel** (value as relation / distance-overcome), **Patinkin** (marginal utility of holding real balances);
  - _substantivist anthropology_ — **Graeber** (debt / credit-first, the barter myth demolished), **Polanyi** (forms of integration), **Mauss** (the gift), **Sahlins** (kinship distance);
  - _the relational nature of money, made popular_ — **Ingham** (_The Nature of Money_) and **Felix Martin** (_Money: The Unauthorised Biography_) — money as a social technology of transferable credit.
- **There is no `Mises` contract — on purpose.** His distinctive idea (the commodity anchor, the regression theorem) is the one thing the framework deliberately does _not_ build. The named absence is the most precise statement of the project: the great subjectivist whose one flinch we refused to instantiate.
- **The cast honours mechanisms, not men.** Several of these men held positions this
  framework exists to refute. Fisher founded the American Eugenics Society and was its
  first president, arguing in cost-benefit terms for sterilising the "unfit"; Commons
  wrote in _Races and Immigrants in America_ (1907) that white races were fitter for
  democracy than tropical ones; Hayek preferred a liberal dictator to an illiberal
  democracy, and said so of Pinochet; Cantillon made his fortune from a scheme he had
  himself called unsound, then foreclosed on the people it ruined. Their mechanisms are
  used here and their premises are not — and the naming is deliberate rather than
  awkward, because essentialism about blood is the same error as essentialism about
  metal. That two of that error's most careful authors are on this list is the argument,
  not an embarrassment to it. Schumpeter's wartime sympathies (contested between Swedberg and McCraw) and
  Friedman's 1975 Pinochet advice (a live argument about an adviser's responsibility,
  not a stated position) belong in the same register with those qualifications attached.
- **`Minsky` is the memento-mori.** The most fragile contract (the risk pool / default waterfall) carries the name of the man who explained why such things detonate — a permanent warning label on the layer that needs it most.
- **Two horizontal concerns** every "clever" fix must face early: a typed claim → challenge → finding lifecycle (with bonded challenge as only one topology), and contextual/selective disclosure so reputation memory does not become surveillance. Neither supplies a social consequence by itself.
- **Two irreducible hard residuals** the cast can de-fang but not dissolve: the inter-community reserve floor (`Keynes`, between truly trustless parties) and decentralized Sybil-resistance (under `ReputationMemory`).
- **Every bend relocates a judgement, it does not remove it.** [`docs/JUDGEMENT-REGISTER.md`](../docs/JUDGEMENT-REGISTER.md) enumerates every point where a written contract stops computing and a person decides — classified **adjudicative / epistemic / normative / constitutive**, each marked _close in code_ or _refer to a named institution_. Its finding: the canon currently routes almost everything to `GovernedDials`, i.e. to a vote, which is the wrong guard for epistemic and constitutive judgements. A register of judgement points is also a register of **capture** points; read it both ways.
