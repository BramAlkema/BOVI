# The Starship Money Crisis: A Choose Your Own Adventure

> **Read with the rebuilt lens (`docs/FOUNDATIONS.md`).** A strong pedagogical vehicle; a couple of framings in it predate the rebuilt foundation, so read it this way. **(1)** All three opening paths land on the *same* thing — a **record of who gave and hasn't yet received** (a ledger). The *Cooperation Path* reaches it most honestly (share → "track contributions" → a record): that's **credit / a ledger first**, exactly as the anthropology finds. The *Innovation Path*'s "cards" are that *same ledger in token form* — the token is the substrate, the **record** is the money. **(2)** Supply is **known, not fixed**: the *Abundant* path (12,000 units) is perfectly fine; scarcity is never the point, *common knowledge of the total* is. **(3)** The four modes are **co-present from the start** (the Cooperation Path opens in Immediate-mode sharing; authority and reciprocity tensions are there on Day 1) — they do **not** "emerge" from money. A money system doesn't *create* the modes; if it ignores them, the suppressed ones **reassert**. Read the "modes emerge" beats below in that light.

**Without money allocation, cooperation is breaking down. You have 72 hours before critical systems fail.**

**You are: Nova, the Systems Integration Specialist**

### **THE FINAL CRISIS: The Suppressed Modes Reassert**

*(The modes were here from Day 1 — the card system just tried to do all four jobs at once and ignored three of them. They don't emerge from the money; they push back against it.)*

**Balanced Mode Crisis**: Luna lends cards to Rio for medical supplies, but tracking "who owes whom" becomes complex outside the simple card system.

**Obligated Mode Crisis**: The ship council demands resource allocation authority for life support systems - creating hierarchy tensions.

**Immediate Mode Crisis**: When Vega gets seriously ill, the crew wants to share resources freely without "transactional thinking."

## 🎯 **ENDING A: THE BOVI INTEGRATION**
*You chose to build fairness modes into the system*

You design the ultimate conscious money system:

```
STARSHIP BOVI MONEY SYSTEM v2.0
═══════════════════════════════════
MODE SELECTOR:
○ Value Mode: Market trades (cards + PDA)
○ Balanced Mode: Time-shifted obligations  
○ Obligated Mode: Authority allocations
○ Immediate Mode: Gift/sharing networks

INTEGRATED FEATURES:
• Minimal money core (cards)
• Price discovery (PDA)  
• Contract stability (SSI)
• Wealth optimization (sweeping)
• Social awareness (mode switching)
```

**The Result**: The crew consciously chooses which fairness mode fits each situation:
- Market trades use cards
- Debts use balanced tracking
- Ship authority uses obligated allocation  
- Family care uses immediate sharing

**You've created the first truly conscious money system!**

**LESSON LEARNED**: Money isn't one thing but a bundle of fairness modes — and they were there from the very first day of sharing, not created by the cards. Good design doesn't *invent* the modes; it stops *suppressing* them, and lets each situation use the one it needs.

---

## 🎯 **ENDING B: THE BUNDLING TRAP**
*You chose to keep one system for everything*

You try to make cards handle all social situations:

- Market trades ✓
- Debt tracking (awkward but works)
- Authority allocation (creates resentment)  
- Gift giving (feels cold and transactional)

**The Result**: The system works but feels wrong. Family relationships become transactional. Authority feels illegitimate when mediated by cards. Gifts lose meaning when tracked.

**6 months later**: Informal systems emerge anyway - people trade outside the card system for social needs.

**LESSON LEARNED**: Trying to force one tool to handle multiple fairness modes creates social friction. Conscious separation works better than forced integration.

---

## 🎯 **ENDING C: THE DUAL ECONOMY**
*You chose to let informal systems handle social needs*

You keep the card system pure and simple, letting social relationships exist in parallel:

**Formal Economy**: Cards + PDA + SSI + Investment pools
**Informal Economy**: Favors, gifts, debt relationships, authority

**The Result**: Works well initially, but creates tension over what belongs in which system. Arguments about "that should be a card transaction" vs "that should be free."

**1 year later**: Boundaries blur anyway. The formal and informal economies influence each other constantly.

**LESSON LEARNED**: You can't cleanly separate economic and social systems. Better to consciously design their interaction than pretend they're independent.
