# Plan: Fusing the Modes and the Money (the "two books" fix)

**Problem.** The book's two spines — *money is a sufficiently-reliable ledger* (Parts II/III/VI) and *exchange runs on four modes* (Parts I/IV/V) — are joined only by assertion ("money is where the modes are most studied"). The modes do almost no work in the money chapters and vice versa; the Tally is the one place they fuse. A structural critic will call it two books stapled.

**Diagnosis.** They were never two subjects. The unifying claim is already present in fragments the book doesn't assemble:

> **A mode is a rule about when and whether the ledger must clear.** Value clears now; Balanced nets over a window; Obligated clears on schedule, with penalty; Immediate never clears — recording it as clearable is the insult. Money is the Value discipline made infrastructural: instant, impersonal, ratio-priced clearing between strangers.

Formal footing (borrowed, not invented — mission-consistent): Fiske's own papers map the four relational models onto the four classic measurement scales — Communal Sharing = nominal, Authority Ranking = ordinal, Equality Matching = interval, Market Pricing = ratio (Fiske 1991; 1992, *Psych. Review*). So the modes are the four possible **resolutions of a ledger**, and the book's two spines are one claim. "Clearing disciplines" is our packaging of Fiske's observation; no new theorem is claimed.

Canon status: FOUNDATIONS Stone 1 already states the mode-general pull ("in every mode the two halves of an exchange come apart… double-coincidence is the *Value-mode acute case*, not the root"). Stone 3 carries the scope guard (origin broad across modes; *priced* value narrow — gifts don't price at a margin). The clearing-discipline identity is in neither canon nor book yet.
