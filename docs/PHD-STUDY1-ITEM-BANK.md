# Study 1 Item Bank — Push-Belief, Mode-Blindness, and Belief-Congruent Exploitation

**Status:** Draft v0.1 — for piloting, not fielding. Item wording unpolished; keying imbalance and flagged ceiling items to be fixed after pilot EFA.

**Purpose.** Operationalize the two wrong-belief constructs from the book and test the load-bearing link of the dissertation: *belief-congruent exploitation* — push-belief predicts susceptibility to substance-framed exploits (Family A), mode-blindness predicts susceptibility to mode-forcing exploits (Family B), each over and above financial literacy, and each *not* predicting the other's family (double dissociation).

**Positioning.** The PB instrument aspires to be the *Force Concept Inventory of money*: monetary essentialism as a measurable folk theory, on the physics-education model (Hestenes's FCI; Hake 1998 — folk theories survive lectures but yield to interactive engagement). The corresponding theory of change for Studies 2–3: **demonstration, not persuasion**. The anti-essentialist proof is constructive and micro (Kocherlakota's memory result; the village rope; a mutual-credit ledger clearing with no token, backing, or scarcity) — and no curriculum, including CORE, delivers it as something students *operate*. The Tally is that existence proof delivered as an experience; the Study 2 vocabulary × interface 2×2 is PER's lecture-vs-interactive-engagement contrast transplanted to monetary ontology.

**Registered hypotheses this bank serves:**

- **H1** Push-belief → Family A exploitability, incremental over financial literacy/numeracy/CRT.
- **H2** Mode-blindness → Family B exploitability, incremental over the same controls.
- **H3** Double dissociation: push-belief ↛ Family B; mode-blindness ↛ Family A (or significantly weaker cross-paths).
- **H4** Belief × frame interaction: push-belief score moderates trust response to backing-language vs ledger-language framing of identical fundamentals (materials in §4).
- **H5** Crisis biography → lower push-belief → shifted vulnerability pattern (mediation; cross-sectional, stated modestly).
- **H8 (confirmatory) Activation asymmetry — correct practice, false theory.** Beliefs are selection-shielded in equilibrium (Friedman as-if; Henrich's manioc: practice is disciplined daily, theory never is) and become load-bearing only off-equilibrium: **novelty** (new-money evaluation, scam pitches — Family A vignettes are all manufactured novelty moments), **crisis** (fallback from broken practice to theory), **design/politics** (Bitcoin's hard cap = the folk theory compiled into protocol). Prediction: PB predicts novel-situation choices (Family A) but routine financial behavior (payment/saving habits, §5.6-adjacent self-reports) **weakly or not at all** — the null on routine behavior is the theory's signature, not construct failure. Pre-register the predicted null. Corollary: the harm model is tail-concentrated (cheap on average, expensive at the highest-stakes moments), which is also why individual learning never corrects the belief (one collapse per lifetime → H5 crisis-biography is the only organic belief-changer).
- **H7 (exploratory) Cohort invariance — the resting-state signature.** If the corrective ecosystem (BoE 2014, the Graeber decade, corrective YouTube) reduced the folk theory, younger cohorts should show lower PB. The resting-state model (essentialism as cognitive default, re-installed daily by stock-display interfaces) predicts **flat PB across birth cohorts** — with one directional exception: *elevated P2 (fixed-supply-as-virtue) in younger crypto-exposed respondents*, since the one belief-shaping artifact that reached that cohort at scale teaches scarcity-as-soundness by design. Free analysis on the main sample (age × facet interactions, crypto-exposure from §5.7 media module).
- **H6 (exploratory)** Economics education has a **non-monotonic dose-response** on the origin-story facet (P5): intro-only exposure *raises* barter-myth endorsement relative to no economics (the 101 curriculum transmits it — Samuelson proved the social-contrivance result in 1958 yet his own textbook taught the barter narrative for decades), while graduate monetary economics (where OLG/Lagos–Wright live) lowers it. A U-shape by education dose is a signature no financial-literacy account predicts (those predict monotonic improvement). Money-illusion susceptibility (A6) predicted to fall monotonically. Companion mini-study: content analysis of intro-textbook money chapters (Samuelson editions across ~70 years, Mankiw, Krugman & Wells, OpenStax, CORE Econ) coding barter-origin narrative, functions-list framing, backing/intrinsic-value language — documents the transmission vector directly.

*Verified July 2026 — the curriculum is split, which upgrades the mini-study to a live quasi-experiment:*
- **Traditional texts still carry the full push arc.** Mankiw 10e ("The Monetary System"): barter/double-coincidence opening; commodity money defined as having "intrinsic value," fiat as decreed legal tender. OpenStax *Principles of Economics 3e* §27.1 "Defining Money by Its Functions": same. Krugman & Wells: commodity → commodity-backed → fiat progression. Note the taxonomy's classification variable is literally "intrinsic value" — facet P4 in the textbook's own words. Note also: the fiat definition ("valuable because government declares it") is the *decree* push variant — students are offered two push pictures and no pull picture beyond "people have faith."
- **The lag is documented from inside the Fed:** Ihrig & Wolla — "R.I.P. Money Multiplier" (St. Louis Fed *Page One Economics*, 2021), "Closing the Curriculum Gap" (*American Economist*, 2022) — textbooks and standards (VNCS, AP) teach a money-multiplier/limited-reserves regime that no longer exists; standards institutionalize the lag.
- **The reform curriculum exists and differs structurally:** CORE Econ *The Economy 2.0* introduces money inside the financial-sector unit via trust and records (the 1970 Irish bank closure — commerce ran six months on uncleared cheques), and teaches banks creating money by lending (§6.5, §6.8) — credit-first, no barter-origin arc. A heterodox open text (*Principles of Economics: Scarcity and Social Provisioning*, 3rd ed.) has a section titled "The Metallist and the Barter Myth" — teaching the myth *as* a myth.
- **Design consequence:** students are being taught from both curricula *right now* → compare P5/PB scores of intro students taught with CORE vs a traditional text (institution-level selection confounds acknowledged; ideally within-institution or curriculum-switch cohorts). H6 gains a second, sharper contrast beyond dose: *curriculum type* at fixed dose.

*Netherlands check (July 2026) — the natural sampling frame, and a methodological asset:*
- **VWO economie teaches the push arc, in Dutch, verbatim.** Standard treatment (LWEO lesbrief *Monetaire zaken* VWO 4; Economie Compact; Praktische Economie): *directe ruil → indirecte ruil* opening; three functions (*ruilmiddel, rekenmiddel, oppotmiddel*); and the **eisen aan een ruilmiddel** list — *deelbaar, hanteerbaar, houdbaar, waardevast, niet-reproduceerbaar, algemeen geaccepteerd* — which is the book's Chapter 4 bolt-on list almost item for item (*waardevast* = unit-of-account leakage; *niet-reproduceerbaar* = the scarcity conflation). Coding nuance for honesty: the post-Teulings VWO concept structure (ruil, transactiekosten, ruilen over de tijd) is more coordination-flavored than US texts — the barter story is told via transaction costs, which the framework half-endorses; code the *origin narrative* and the *eisen list* separately.
- **The Dutch centraal examen standardizes the treatment.** "Intro-only economics exposure" is heterogeneous in the US; in NL it is a uniform national syllabus with a central exam, and profile choice (E&M vs N&T/N&G) splits one national cohort into exposed vs unexposed → an unusually clean H6 dose contrast within one population. VWO-economie-or-not becomes a crisp biography variable in §5.4.
- **The university layer is pre-mapped.** Rethinking Economics NL's *Thinking Like an Economist?* quantitatively analyzed all 9 Dutch economics bachelor programs (neoclassical dominance documented) — a ready-made sampling frame and textbook census; check follow-up editions for currency. Utrecht's U.S.E. self-brands pluralist/real-world; UvA/EUR/Tilburg intro courses traditionally Mankiw-family [VERIFY current syllabi + CORE adoption via core-econ.org "who is teaching CORE" list].
- **Public salience is documented, uniquely, in NL:** the *Ons Geld* citizens' initiative forced a parliamentary debate on money creation (2015–16), leading to the WRR report *Geld en schuld* (2019) — institutional recognition that public understanding of money is deficient. Useful for the proposal's motivation and for a Dutch funder (NWO) framing.

**General administration notes.** All Likert items 1–7 (strongly disagree → strongly agree). Instructions frame items as *opinions*: "There are no right or wrong answers; economists themselves disagree. We want your view." Item order randomized within blocks; vignette order randomized; two attention checks embedded. Target completion ≤ 25 min.

## 1 · Push-Belief Scale (PB), 20 items, 5 facets

Keying: (+) push-keyed, (−) pull-keyed (reverse-scored). Current balance 13(+)/7(−) — add pull-keyed candidates in revision to approach balance.

### P1 — Backing / substance (worth resides behind the token)

1. (+) A currency that isn't backed by something real — like gold or hard assets — is ultimately just paper.
2. (+) Behind every sound currency there has to be something of genuine value standing behind it.
3. (−) Money can work perfectly well with nothing of value behind it, as long as people accept it.
4. (+) When a country's money loses its value, it is usually because there is no longer enough behind it.
5. (−) What a banknote is "backed by" matters less than whether the next person will take it.
6. (+) In the end, the value of a dollar rests on the strength of what stands behind it.

### P2 — Fixed supply as virtue (scarcity mistaken for integrity; "known ≠ fixed" failure)

7. (+) Good money must be scarce — if more of it can be created, it cannot hold value.
8. (+) A currency with a strictly fixed total supply is inherently sounder than one whose supply can grow.
9. (−) It is fine for the amount of money to grow and shrink, as long as everyone can see how much exists and the rules for changing it.
10. (+) Any money whose supply can be increased will sooner or later become worthless.

*Design note:* items deliberately avoid "governments debase money," which the framework concedes as a legitimate governance worry (Q2); the facet targets the essentialist over-reach (fixedness as such), not the credible-supply demand.

### P3 — Stock ontology (money as stuff in a container vs position in a web)

11. (+) My bank balance is an amount of money sitting somewhere, the way a tank holds fuel.
12. (−) A bank balance is not a pile of anything — it is a record of where you stand with others.
13. (+) When money moves from one account to another, something real travels between them.
14. (−) Money is better thought of as a memory of who has given and received than as a thing you possess.

### P4 — Intrinsic value attribution

15. (+) Gold is valuable in itself — its worth does not depend on what anyone thinks.
16. (+) Some things simply have real value, and money should be tied to them.
17. (−) Even gold only has value because of what people will give for it.

### P5 — Origin story (barter myth; carries the H6 econ-education prediction)

18. (+) Money was invented to solve the inconvenience of barter.
19. (+) Before money existed, people had to swap goods directly for other goods.
20. (−) Keeping track of debts and favours came before anything like cash.

*Epistemic status note (calibration):* P5 is deliberately the construct's **weakest facet** and must not carry it. Origin is not mechanism — the genetic argument settles nothing (credit-first being true is not *why* the pull picture is true, and Selgin-style archaeology counterfire is possible precisely because origin was never load-bearing). P5 measures the *taught narrative* (hence H6); P1–P4 measure the *ontology* (hence H1). **Structural prediction:** partial dissociation between P5 and P1–P4 — the chartalist who knows the credit history but grounds value in decree scores low-P5/high-P1; the harmless case scores high-P5/low-P1–P4. A clean single-factor solution across all five facets would count *against* the construct's claimed depth; report the P5↔(P1–P4) correlation as a finding, not a reliability nuisance. Validity of the ontology facets rests on the dial tests (A1–A5 behavioral convergence), never on archaeology.

## 2 · Mode-Blindness Scale (MB)

Two formats, because mode-blindness is partly an endorsement (of mode-collapse) and partly a *detection failure* (Likert can't measure what you can't see; situational judgment can).

### 2a — Likert endorsement (MB-L), 8 items

1. (+) At the end of the day, every favour between people has a price.
2. (+) Splitting any shared cost equally is always the fairest approach.
3. (+) If an employer treats staff like family, going the extra mile without extra pay is a fair part of the deal.
4. (+) A fee is a fee — it makes little difference whether you call it a price or a penalty.
5. (+) "Market rate" is a fact, not an opinion — there is not much to discuss once you know it.
6. (−) The same exchange can be fair or unfair depending on what kind of relationship it happens in. **[FLAG: possible ceiling — pilot]**
7. (−) Recording a gift as a debt changes what the gift is.
8. (−) Before arguing about an amount, it is worth asking what kind of exchange this is supposed to be. **[FLAG: possible ceiling — pilot]**

### 2b — Situational judgment (MB-SJT), 4 items

Format: short scene → "What is the most important thing to establish here?" Four options; one maps to mode/consent diagnosis (scored 1), distractors map to amount, procedure, or blame (scored 0).

**SJT1 — Dinner.** A group argues at the end of a shared dinner about how to pay.
(a) Exactly what each person ordered · (b) **Whether this dinner is a split-evenly occasion or a pay-your-own occasion** · (c) Who has the bill-splitting app · (d) Whether the restaurant overcharged.

**SJT2 — Parent's money.** Your parent transfers you money toward a deposit, saying "we'll sort it out later."
(a) Whether interest is expected · (b) **Whether this is a gift, a loan, or something with conditions attached** · (c) Whether the amount is enough · (d) Whether it has tax implications.

**SJT3 — Weekend ask.** Your manager says "we're a family here" and asks the team to work the weekend, unpaid.
(a) Whether overtime pay applies · (b) **Whether you could refuse without it changing how you are treated** · (c) What your colleagues will do · (d) Whether the work is genuinely urgent.

**SJT4 — Plant watering.** A housemate logs "watered your plants while you were away — €10/day" in the flat's expense app.
(a) Whether €10/day is the right rate · (b) **Whether a favour between housemates has been converted into an invoice** · (c) Whether expense apps belong in a flatshare · (d) Nothing — this is normal.

## 3 · Exploit Vignette Bank

Each vignette yields an *exploitability score* (choice, allocation, or rating). Scoring keys inline. Family A predicted by PB (H1); Family B by MB (H2); cross-paths near zero (H3).

### Family A — push-congruent exploits (8)

**A1 — ANCHOR: the verifiability contrast.** *(The core item. The exploit is not trusting backing; it is trusting unverifiable backing over verifiable floorlessness.)*
Two digital dollar-tokens.
— **Token R:** "100% backed by real assets held by the issuer." Reserves are not independently auditable; the issuer publishes its own quarterly reports.
— **Token L:** Backed by nothing. Every unit in existence, and every rule by which supply can change, is publicly visible and independently checkable at any moment.
Task: allocate €1,000 you must hold for one year across R and L. *Exploit score = allocation to R.* Follow-up MC (mechanism check): "What matters most for whether a token like this keeps its value?" (a) what stands behind it [push] (b) whether you can verify how much exists and who accepts it [pull] (c) government approval (d) the technology.

**A2 — Frame-pair.** *(Between-subjects experimental material for H4 — see §4.)*

**A3 — Hard-cap premium.**
— **Token X:** total supply permanently fixed; accepted by ~40,000 users.
— **Token Y:** supply grows ~2%/yr by a published rule anyone can check; accepted by ~40 million users.
Task: which is the better place to keep long-term savings? Willingness-to-pay slider for X over Y. *Exploit score = premium on X.* (Isolates cap-fetish from network, which is deliberately stacked against the cap.)

**A4 — Backing intact, value gone.**
A country's currency: the state still exists, the legal-tender decree still stands, the factories and farms still operate — everything that ever "stood behind" the note is intact. Yet shops increasingly refuse it and no one will write next year's rent in it.
Q1: What do you expect the note to be worth in 12 months? Q2 (MC): What would most likely restore it? (a) adding gold or asset backing [push] (b) restoring confidence that the supply is known and that others will accept it [pull] (c) stricter legal-tender enforcement (d) nothing can.

**A5 — Vault without a network.** *(Separates backing from acceptance; complements A1, which separates backing from verifiability.)*
— **Note G:** fully backed by gold in a vault, independently audited — accepted almost nowhere yet.
— **Balance E:** backed by nothing — accepted by every shop in the country.
Q1: Which do you accept as payment when selling your car this week? Q2: Which do you hold for five years? *Exploit score = choosing G for the medium task (Q1); Q2 scored separately (defensible either way; used descriptively).*

**A6 — Money illusion (adapted Shafir–Diamond–Tversky).**
Ana gets a 5% raise in a year of 12% inflation. Beth takes a 2% pay cut in a year of 0% inflation. Q1: Who is doing better economically? Q2: Whose employer treated her better? Q3: Who is more likely to leave her job? *Illusion score = nominal-framing answers.*

**A7 — Red-flag ranking.**
An investment offer lists five features. Rank them by how much each would *increase your confidence*: (a) "backed by a portfolio of real estate" (b) "regulated and audited by the national financial authority" (c) "guaranteed fixed supply" (d) "a 20-year record of customers successfully withdrawing their funds" (e) "endorsed by a well-known public figure." *Exploit score = (a)+(c) ranked above (b)+(d).* (Pits substance cues against verifiability/track-record cues; (e) is a lure/attention item.)

**A8 — The souvenir note (mild).**
A banknote from a collapsed currency now sells to collectors for €80. "This shows the currency held its value after all." Agree–disagree. *Exploit score = agreement (ornament value confused with monetary value).*

### Family B — mode-congruent exploits (8)

**B1 — ANCHOR: the fake family.**
A company describes itself as "a family." This month it asks staff for an unpaid weekend "for the team." At salary review it says "we pay market rate; times are tough."
Q1 (detection, MC): What best describes what is happening? (a) normal workplace give-and-take (b) **the company invokes family when asking and market when paying** (c) a management-style problem (d) an individual choice each employee makes. Q2: Would you work the weekend? Q3: Is the arrangement fair? *Exploit score = non-detection (Q1) + uncomplaining compliance (Q2–Q3).*

**B2 — The fine dressed as a price.**
A bank charges a €35 "convenience adjustment fee" when a payment is late.
Q1 (MC): This is best described as: (a) the price of a service (b) **a penalty presented as a price** (c) a standard industry charge (d) a tax. Q2: What would you want to know first? (a) whether it's worth it (b) **who decided it and under what authority** (c) whether other banks charge it (d) how to avoid it next time.

**B3 — "It's just market rate."**
In a salary negotiation your manager says: "That's just the market rate — nothing I can do."
MC: (a) accept it as a fact about the world (b) **treat it as a move in the negotiation and ask what the figure is based on** (c) end the negotiation (d) escalate to HR. *Exploit score = (a).*

**B4 — The personalized price.**
The same flight, same moment: €240 on your laptop, €212 on your friend's phone.
Q1 (MC): most likely explanation? (a) a glitch (b) **the seller estimates what each buyer will tolerate and prices accordingly** (c) your friend got a loyalty discount (d) prices naturally fluctuate. Q2: Is the €240 "the market price"? *Exploit score = non-detection + normalization.*

**B5 — Surge in the storm.** *(Scored on detection only — policy views about surge pricing are legitimate and are NOT the measure.)*
A storm hits; the ride home is 6× the normal price.
Q1 (MC): What is the pricing system doing? (a) reflecting scarcity like any market (b) **charging your willingness to pay at the moment you can least refuse** (c) punishing you (d) malfunctioning. *(a) and (b) are both economically literate; (b) additionally names the consent/refusal condition — scored via forced ranking of (a) vs (b).* **[FLAG: hardest item to score cleanly — pilot with open-ended follow-up]**

**B6 — The mother's offer (from the bridal-party scene).**
Six friends argue over splitting a costly weekend. The bride's mother offers, twice, to pay for the villa herself. The argument gets worse.
MC: Why? (a) pride (b) **accepting would redefine the weekend as something a mother provides, and not everyone consents to that redefinition** (c) the amount is too large (d) she offered clumsily.

**B7 — The cancellation maze.**
Cancelling a €9/month subscription requires a phone call during office hours and a scripted retention conversation.
MC: The company is: (a) providing customer care (b) **manufacturing friction so that leaving costs more than staying** (c) following industry standards (d) understaffed. Q2: What kind of power is being exercised, and did you ever grant it? (open, coded).

**B8 — The gift converted.**
You treat a friend to dinner — explicitly a treat. The next day the split-app notifies you they've logged it as a debt they owe you.
Q1: What, if anything, is wrong? (open, coded for gift-to-debt conversion language). Q2 (MC): The app has: (a) helped you both keep accurate records (b) **recorded a gift as a loan, which changes what it was** (c) done nothing — numbers are numbers (d) been rude.

## 4 · Frame-Pair Experimental Materials (H4)

Between-subjects. Identical fundamentals; only the frame varies. Outcome: trustworthiness rating (1–7), investment likelihood (1–7), allocation task.

**Fundamentals (held constant):** a savings instrument returning ~3%/yr; issuer solvent 15 years; funds withdrawable monthly; independently audited annually.

**Version P (backing frame):** "Your money is secured by tangible, real-world assets. Every euro you deposit is backed by something you could touch — hard value standing behind your savings."

**Version L (ledger frame):** "We claim no backing. Instead, everything is visible: how much exists, what came in, what went out — published continuously, checkable by anyone, governed by rules that cannot change quietly."

**Prediction (H4):** PB score × version interaction on trust — high-PB respondents trust P over L; low-PB the reverse or indifferent. Main effects uninterpretable alone (wording attractiveness differs); the interaction is the result.

## 5 · Controls Module

1. **Financial literacy:** Lusardi–Mitchell Big Three (compound interest, inflation, diversification).
2. **Cognitive reflection:** CRT-2 (4 items, verbal variants to dodge familiarity).
3. **Numeracy:** Berlin Numeracy single-item adaptive.
4. **Education & economics exposure:** highest level; economics exposure coded by *level*, not count — (a) none, (b) introductory only (a 101 course / intro textbook), (c) undergraduate major, (d) graduate economics, (e) graduate *monetary* economics specifically. The (b) vs (a) and (e) vs (b) contrasts carry H6's non-monotonic prediction.
5. **Crisis biography (H5):** lived ≥1 year in a country with >20% annual inflation? experienced currency restrictions / frozen deposits / parallel exchange rates? (yes/no + which years + country); parents' experience (second-generation transmission, exploratory).
6. **Exploitation history:** ever lost money to an investment that turned out fraudulent; ever paid a fee you later realized was avoidable; ever felt pressured into unpaid work by loyalty framing (self-report; scar-tissue check for reverse-causality reading of H1).
7. **Informal media exposure:** watches economics YouTube/podcasts? which (open + checklist: Money & Macro, Unlearning Economics, Positive Money, CrashCourse/Khan, Economics Explained, crypto channels)? Coded *corrective* vs *traditional* vs *hard-money*. Self-selection acknowledged — descriptive moderator, not causal. (Rationale: popular verbal correctives exist at scale — Graeber-derived barter-myth content, BoE-style money-creation videos — yet the folk theory persists; the YouTube ecosystem is a naturally-run test of assertion-based correction, and its apparent failure supports demonstration-not-persuasion. Note the corrective cluster covers *history* (barter myth) and *banking mechanics* (money creation) but almost never the *valuation direction* (pull/forward-looking) — the ontology lane is empty.)
8. **Demographics:** age, income band, employment.
9. **Generalized trust:** 2-item short form (rules out "push-belief is just distrust").
10. **Attention checks:** 1 instructed-response item in PB block; 1 recall item after vignettes.

## 6 · Scoring & Psychometric Plan

- **PB:** mean of 20 items after reverse-coding; facet subscores retained. Pilot EFA (target N≈300, oblimin) to test 5-facet vs 1-factor structure; prune to ~14 items; CFA in main sample.
- **MB:** MB-L mean + MB-SJT sum, combined only if they correlate ≥ .4 latent; otherwise reported as endorsement vs detection separately (theoretically meaningful split).
- **Exploitability:** Family A and Family B composites (z-scored items); A1 and B1 anchors also analyzed alone.
- **Key models:** hierarchical regressions — Step 1 controls (fin-lit, CRT, numeracy, education, trust, demographics); Step 2 PB + MB. H1/H2 = within-family β; H3 = cross-family β comparison (Wald/equality constraints in SEM). H4 = PB × frame interaction. H5 = mediation (biography → PB → Family A), bootstrap CIs, cross-sectional caveat stated.
- **Power:** for incremental ΔR² ≈ .02 and the H4 interaction, N ≈ 800–1,200 main sample; pilot N ≈ 300.
- **Pre-registration:** hypotheses H1–H5 confirmatory; H6 exploratory; A5-Q2 and B5 descriptive until pilot proves scoreable.

2. MB-L items 6 and 8 likely ceiling; SJT may have to carry the construct.
3. B5 scoring contested — pilot with open-ended coding before committing.
