# The Foundations — money from first principles, stone by stone

*The rebuilt foundation beneath `CANON.md`. Where the 20 Quirks are corrections to the standard treatment, this is the **derivation** the corrections rest on: what money is, what it's worth, what a substrate must have, and where it tends — each step drop-tested, none asserted.*

## What this document is (and is not)

This is **premise-axiomatisation of one school** — the relational / subjectivist / substantivist account of money — made explicit, independent, and sufficiency-checked, *with the rival schools named, not assumed away*. It is **not** a tidying of a settled field (monetary economics has no agreed Euclid; its content is contested across Austrian, Keynesian, MMT, New-Monetarist, chartalist lines). It adds **no new theorems** — every conclusion is borrowed from the giants and *assembled*. Its contribution is the assembly, the explicit premises, and the teaching.

Posture, inherited from the canon: **theoretically partisan** (strong positions on what money *is* and how value works — that's this document) and **politically neutral** (no position on which mode or policy should win — that stays the reader's). A tool for *seeing*, micro-first, aimed at the person being taxed rather than the one at the lever-panel.

**Epistemic status of the axiom set (Stone 4):** necessity ✓ (each drop-tested), independence ✓ (pairwise), **sufficiency argued, not proven** (by elimination of the usual extras + a working minimal instance; you cannot enumerate every conceivable missing ingredient). Claims are stated at their actual strength — no tidy wrapper above, no false modesty below.

## Stone 1 — the pull

> Money is **pulled** into existence by the need for a reliable **memory of who has given and not yet received** — because giving and receiving separate, across counterparties and across time. It is not **pushed** into value by anything it is made of.

In every mode the two halves of an exchange come apart: a favour owed back later (Balanced), tribute rendered for standing (Obligated), help returned someday, a market trade whose wants don't coincide (Value). The **double-coincidence-of-wants** is just this non-coincidence at its sharpest — the *Value-mode acute case*, not the root.

*Drop-test:* remove the pull and money's value must come from what it's made of — folk-physics essentialism, which fails for fiat, for credit, and for a valueless tally.

*Root posture:* the first question of any money is never "what backs it" (push) but "what separation of give-and-receive does it let people bridge" (pull).

## Stone 2 — the ledger (the mechanism, derived)

The minimal object that holds "who has given and not received" is a list of net positions — a **ledger**. So what the pull reaches for is a *ledger of who-owes-whom = **credit***. "Money is memory" — derived, not posited, and carried at the theorem's own strength: Kocherlakota's result is an *inclusion* (anything money achieves, memory achieves — money is his "primitive form of memory"), with the converse failing precisely in stranger-matching, where memory dominates.

*Drop-test:* a pure commodity valued for its substance cannot hold who-gave-and-didn't-receive, so it leaves the pull unanswered. Only a ledger answers Stone 1.

**The record landing (a result, not a patch):** the pulled-in object is a **record of obligation**, and that is what the evidence shows came first — stratified, because the strata differ and the difference matters. Institutional accounting from **~3400 BCE** (proto-cuneiform, Uruk IV/III: rations, disbursements, herds, personnel — roughly 85% bookkeeping, and **no loan instruments**); interest-bearing obligation attested by **~2400 BCE** (Enmetena's *amargi* decree cancels "all interest due," and you cannot cancel interest that does not exist); an institutional lending system in **Ur III** (~2110–2003), which the responsible Assyriologist reads as a granary-and-food-security instrument for state dependents rather than a market in credit; and a genuine **loan-contract genre with bearer clauses** in Old Babylonian and Old Assyrian (~1900–1600). Coinage arrives ~630–600 BCE — after all of it.

*This document previously said "the pulled-in object is credit… debt before coin. No reconciliation needed," and audited 2026-08 that overstates the fourth millennium.* **Credit** in the transferable-instrument sense is a second-millennium development. What the earliest stratum supplies is a **ledger** — which is precisely what the line above derives, so the correction strengthens the stone rather than costing it: the record vindicates the derivation and only embarrassed the gloss. *(This project had already reached the same conclusion three separate times — in its origin-stories lineage doc, in the recension's "money is debt's antidote, not its embodiment," and in the Yap case's "a title registry is not a ledger" — and had not propagated it here.)*

**And say what is still open.** The negative result is secure: no barter origin (Humphrey 1985: *"No example of a barter economy, pure and simple, has ever been described, let alone the emergence from it of money"*). The positive result is a record of obligation, as above. *Which mode that record served* — reciprocity, redistribution, or an early tax-drive — is genuinely unsettled, and Stone 1 does not need it settled, because the pull is mode-general: a redistributive ledger still holds who has given and not yet received. A framework that required the credit reading would owe that fight; this one does not.

**The identity (the sharp claim):** a sufficiently-divisible, symmetrically-known *good* **is** that ledger — holdings = balances; a good moving A→B = one entry (physical conservation enforces double-entry for free); divisibility = resolution; symmetrically-known total = integrity. So commodity money was *always ledger money in costume*. There was no commodity→abstract transition — only the **substrate getting thinner** (cattle → metal → coin → note → bank-ledger → chain).

**Known ≠ fixed:** the commodity ledger only *looks* like it needs a fixed total; that fixity is the substrate being conserved, not a requirement. Mutual credit (signed balances, zero net, elastic gross) is the same ledger with the substrate removed, the fixity dropped — **and the floor at zero lifted, which is not nothing**: the token form's balances cannot go negative, and that floor is both its limit (no credit) and its enforcement (possession *is* settlement; nobody can owe — experimentally, the floor itself disciplines free-riding: Bigoni–Camera–Casari 2020). Lifting it is what re-opens the question Stone 4 prices as *no floor, pay with teeth*.

**The modes as clearing disciplines (a frame, not a stone):** a mode is a rule about *when and whether the ledger must clear*. Value clears now; Balanced nets over a window; Obligated clears on schedule, with penalty; Immediate never clears — recording it as clearable is the insult. The footing is a borrowed *analogy* — Fiske's own, drawn in passing and never formalised or tested: he compares the four relational models to the four classic measurement scales (Communal Sharing = nominal, Authority Ranking = ordinal, Equality Matching = interval, Market Pricing = ratio — Fiske 1991; 1992 *Psych. Review*; its nesting corollary failed the one direct test, Dien et al. 2018). Read through it, the modes are the four **resolutions a ledger can keep**, and money is the ratio/instant-clearing discipline made infrastructural. Consistent with Stone 1 (the pull is mode-general; double-coincidence is the Value-acute case) and Stone 3's scope guard (gifts don't price at a margin). *Status:* packaging of Fiske, no new theorem. *Drop-test:* remove the frame and Stones 1–6 stand unchanged — it carries exposition (the money story and the mode story are one object), not load. Complements `EXTRACTION-AND-THE-MODES.md`, where the same four modes appear as extraction's *costumes*: this frame says what a mode is mechanically; that one says how extraction dresses in them.

## Stone 3 — the value: floorless but fruitful

> The ledger is worth exactly the cooperation it unlocks that couldn't happen without it — **nothing prior underneath it (floorless), real surplus inside it (fruitful)**.

*Scope (origin broad, measured value narrow):* the origin is general across modes, but *priced* value is trade-centric (Value/Balanced); gifts don't price at a margin, so we don't claim money's worth comes from enabling them.

**Three positions, not two:** floor (Mises/metallism — essentialism, refused) · pure bubble (Samuelson/Tirole — floorless, and a store of value that mediates nothing) · **floorless-but-fruitful** (ours — floorless yet real, because the payoff is generated *by* acceptance). The crux is **prior**: a floor exists before acceptance; our value exists only because the ledger is accepted — **inside the loop, not under it.** *Ours is not the middle position between floor and bubble but a distinct third one, and Tirole 1985 §6 is why: a money with a lasting transaction role cannot carry a bubble term, so "floorless plus fruitful" is his* fundamentalist *case, not his bubbly one. The consistency proof for a transaction rail comes from the search-theoretic line (Kiyotaki–Wright, Lagos–Wright), not from OLG.*

**Two fruits:** (1) *transactional* — surplus on trades the ledger directly mediates (Patinkin/Lagos–Wright convenience yield); (2) *option / specialisation* — the division of labour that the **expected availability** of the ledger underwrites, accruing to people who never hold it (Smith's extent-of-the-market). The second dwarfs the first.

**Two legs:** value needs the expectation of **acceptance** (the money is good — selection) *and* **availability** (a market is there — extent). They fail separately (good money/thin market; liquid market/bad money). They *gate* the value; they are not the value.

**Flow, not stock:** value is realised only while the ledger circulates. A floor (store-value loaded into the medium) chokes circulation → delivers *less* value. No-floor is therefore *functionally optimal*, not merely honest.

**Complete intersubjectivist refinement:** remove the remaining temptation to treat “social value” as one shared valuation. Acceptance is recursively intersubjective: each participant partly accepts the ledger because they expect other participants and their counterparties to accept it. The resulting price is not the sum of everyone’s beliefs and not an intrinsic social substance. It is a socially stabilised exchange ratio reflecting the holder’s expected **marginal net service**—direct trades, network access, specialisation options, and uncertainty protection—less transaction costs, monetary-layer costs, risk, and extraction. Public benefits and harms may remain outside the price unless they feed back into acceptance, availability, credibility, supply expectations, or the holder’s marginal position. Thus **intersubjectivity explains the loop; marginal surplus explains the price; power explains the distribution.**

In schematic form:

`money price ≈ PV(expected marginal monetary benefits − marginal costs − risk − extraction)`

This is a decomposition, not a claim that agents perform the calculation explicitly. “Price” remains relational: money has no absolute price apart from an exchange ratio against goods, labour, assets, or another monetary unit. Material scarcity, production, time, technology, law, and institutional capacity constrain the relation without supplying an intrinsic monetary floor.

*Drop-test (both ways):* drop *fruitful* → a pure bubble (can't explain selection or specialisation); drop *floorless* → essentialism + a choked flow.

*Lineage (borrowed = strength):* Simmel (value = relation/distance-overcome) + Patinkin (marginal utility of holding) + Lagos–Wright (microfoundation), with Samuelson/Tirole the consistency proof and the contrast.

## Stone 4 — the axioms (what a substrate must be)

Run the candidate properties through the independence test and they resolve into **two families, four requirements** — plus what the *world* must supply.

**Family A — substrate:**
- **A1 Resolution** (*sufficiently* divisible) — represents the entries trades need. *Drop →* lumpy, trades throttle. Free for an abstract ledger.

**Family B — integrity (no phantom value); three independent requirements, one per attack surface:**
- **B1 Conservation** (zero-sum) — a transfer can't mint. *(A binary invariant: enforced, or the ledger is broken.)*
- **B2 Authentication** (*sufficiently* verifiable) — a unit can't be forged.
- **B3 Symmetrically-known supply** (*sufficiently* known) — the issuer can't dilute in secret. **Known ≠ fixed:** auditable and symmetric, not frozen; elastic supply that moves *visibly and by rule* keeps integrity. Scarcity is only a crude, rigid over-satisfaction of B3.

*(Transferability is inherited from Stone 2 — a ledger you can't write to isn't one.)*

**The setting (world-supplied, not substrate properties):**
- **Acceptance** (selection) and **availability** (extent) — Stone 3's two legs.
- **Enforcement — the *credit form's* residual:** why a debtor settles. A commodity ledger discharges this at the substrate (possession *is* settlement) but pays with a **floor**; the signed-balance *credit* form drops the floor and takes on debts that need **teeth** (reputation, recourse, collateral — Greif). *No floor, pay with teeth.* We relocate the hard problem from *what backs it* to *why it's honoured*; we don't dissolve it.

**"Sufficiently" is a doorway, not a hedge — the skims live in the gaps.** Each spectrum requirement (A1, B2, B3) is a *threshold*; the shortfall between *sufficient* and *perfect* houses a characteristic skim:

| requirement | type | the gap below perfect houses |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution | spectrum | rounding / minimum-denomination |
| Authentication | spectrum | counterfeiting + verification cost |
| Known supply/terms | spectrum | **spreads / opacity (terms)** — see below for why *supply* moved out |
| Conservation | binary invariant | — no gap |

Extraction of the **terms** kind follows information asymmetry, so it pools in the known-ness gap — which is why `Stigler` is a known-ness detector: price dispersion under search cost is an information phenomenon through and through. **`Cantillon` is not, and this document had it misfiled** (audited 2026-08). Read his own text: the losers are *"les Propriétaires des terres, **pendant le terme de leurs Baux**"* and *"tous les ouvriers ou gens **à gages fixes**"* — landowners during the term of their leases, workers on fixed wages. The binding variable is a **fixed nominal claim meeting a moving price level**, not anybody's ignorance of the money supply; his own worked example, the Spanish American silver, was public knowledge. The framework's flagship modern instance says the same thing louder: **QE was pre-announced in size, pace and asset class** — perfect known-ness on B3 — and redistributed anyway. If this were a known-ness phenomenon it would have produced none.

So the incidence belongs on the **unit-of-account / nominal-contract** axis, which this document already states correctly under Stone 6: inflation revalues the unit and with it *every nominal contract denominated in it*. That is where the well-evidenced literature lives too — inflation as a regressive consumption tax (Erosa–Ventura; Albanesi), and the redistribution of nominal wealth by unexpected inflation (Doepke–Schneider). What survives of the sequencing intuition survives under other names and with real identification: lagged price adjustment to monetary injections (Palma on the American treasure, *REStud* 2021) and network position determining who is reached first (Ozdagli–Weber). Cite those when evidentiary weight is wanted. The requirements guarantee *function*, not *fairness*; the gaps are the fairness battleground.

**The anti-essentialist result, as a checklist:** the substrate requirements are exactly the **ledger** properties. Scarcity, durability, intrinsic worth, backing appear in *neither family* — everything the goldbug, cryptopoof, and nationalist point to as "what makes it real" fails to appear in the requirements at all.

## Stone 5 — the bolt-ons (the negations)

Run every "essential" that isn't an axiom through the double test — **neither necessary nor sufficient** — and sort by *why* it fails. Three fates:

**Redundant** (crude over-satisfaction of an axiom): *scarcity* → B3 (rare stamps aren't money) · *durability* → B2-over-time (granite isn't money) · *portability* → transferability (bank money never moves; Yap's rai never did — multi-tonne stones changed owners by public acknowledgment alone).

**Misplaced** (a real function, but it belongs unbundled in its own tool — Quirk 14): *store of value* → productive assets (gold in a vault doesn't circulate) · *unit of account* → an index/PDA (transact in pesos, account in USD) · *stability* → the unit (a price index is stable, not a medium).

**Nothing** (essentialist fiction, does no work): *backing* (fiat works unbacked; gold-scrip nobody accepts isn't money) · *intrinsic/use value* (diamonds aren't money) · *the token itself* (mutual credit has none; arcade tokens are money only inside their acceptance network — the ledger, not the token).

**This pays down the sufficiency debt:** every "you also need X" turns out redundant, misplaced, or nothing. With a **working minimal instance** (`Kocherlakota.sol` — the axioms and *none of the bolt-ons*, functioning), sufficiency is **argued** (elimination + existence proof), though not deductively closed. Audited 2026-08 and stated at that strength: conservation holds on every write path and the instance checks itself (`netSupply()` must read 0); resolution and authentication hold; **known-supply strains in three named gaps** — an external elasticity oracle that moves credit limits without a local event, retroactive demurrage, and stale balances between touches. None break conservation; all three redistribute *gross* claims, which is this Stone's own prediction rather than a defect in it. What the instance does *not* show is that the axioms suffice **without a governance layer**: it needs one, and Stone 4 says why — lifting the token's floor at zero removes the enforcement that possession-is-settlement supplied, so the credit limit is the teeth, not an extra.

*Anti-essentialist clincher:* gold *looked* essential because it carried the whole bolt-on package at once (scarce *and* durable *and* useful *and* a token). Strip them one at a time and the ledger is still standing, still working. The substance was always the bolt-on; the ledger was always the money.

## Stone 6 — the medium/store tradeoff (of which "melt" is one pole)

Not a prescription ("money should melt") but an **unavoidable tradeoff** in the design space. A good *store* rewards holding (→ hoarded → bad medium); a good *medium* rewards parting-with (→ poor hold → bad store). Holding and parting-with are opposite dispositions toward the same object, so **store-quality and medium-quality are negatively coupled in one instrument** — Quirk 14 made rigorous via the two-stroke circulation engine. You can't co-optimise; so **unbundle** (a melting medium *plus* a separate appreciating store — nobody loses the store, it just isn't the rail).

**"Melt" = engineered not to be a hoardable store**, two mechanisms: *demurrage* (Gesell — a cost on holding-duration, biting hoarders, sparing transactors) and *elasticity* (known ≠ fixed — no scarcity-appreciation). The inversion of the hard-money design, which engineers *appreciation* → store → hoarding → a bad medium (the deflation engine).

**The honesty calibration** — demurrage and the inflation tax both make held money lose value, and the audited comparison keeps less of the old contrast than we claimed but gains a stronger leg. What survives: demurrage is *known* (the stamp is on the note; the rate and threshold are pre-announced — inflation's realised rate is known only ex post) and *rule-bound with the caveat that the rule is the issuer's to change* (Wörgl's council set it; Chiemgauer cut its own rate; Danish banks walked thresholds down — visibility differs from inflation's governance exposure, kind does not). What does not survive: "avoidable" discriminates nothing (both are avoided by spending or holding something else), and "symmetric" fails — demurrage has a first receiver by construction (Wörgl's parish took the stamp revenue), so the honest phrase is **disclosed Cantillon**, not absent Cantillon. The strongest distinction is one we had not claimed: demurrage is a levy on a *stock of the medium*, while inflation revalues the *unit of account* and with it **every nominal contract denominated in it** — the economy-wide creditor-to-debtor transfer is the inflation tax's alone (the gap narrows against anticipated inflation, and is widest against surprise). And one measured cost of the honesty itself: the single retail implementation of a known, pre-announced holding cost (Denmark, 2019–21) produced *loss aversion, perceived unfairness, and irritation* — legibility earned anger, not consent. Honest, and not thereby popular.

*Honest costs, on the audited record:* it's the Gesellian/relational school's design (rivals reject it). **Wörgl is small and its causal numbers are undocumented** — roughly 5,500–6,000 schillings circulating in a town of 4,250, works funded by provincial subsidies and back-tax collection, and the famous velocity figures trace to the mayor's own report relayed by Fisher's proxy, with the contemporaneous chronicler von Muralt saying at the time he could not verify them (its *legal* fate — killed by the note monopoly, VwGH A 504/33 — is solid, and that is the bearer-vs-book fact, untouched). **The retail-scale test has now actually run**: Denmark 2019–21, negative rates reaching about two-thirds of personal deposits threshold-first — biting hoarders, sparing transactors, exactly this design — and the treated households responded in the Gesellian direction (deposits down, most of it into consumption, no cash-hoarding surge), on the strength of one unrefereed study; **depth was never tested** (−0.6%/year against Wörgl's 12%). **Keynes's substitution objection stands, measured**: about a third of the Danish outflow went into stocks and pensions, and every published route to closing that leak (Goodfriend, Buiter, Agarwal–Kimball) requires breaking cash–deposit par or abolishing cash — sovereign powers a voluntary melt does not have. **The bank channel has a floor** (Brunnermeier–Koby's reversal rate: below it, the levy contracts credit). **Depth is an adoption constraint** (Chiemgauer cut its own melt from 8% to 6%; Freicoin, melt-by-design with no incumbent unit to ride, found no users at all). And it **relocates friction onto the user** (manage the stack — Doña Elena's "exhausting" five layers).

*Drop-test:* make the medium a fixed, appreciating store → hoarded → *willing-to-part-with-it* collapses → it fails as a medium (Bitcoin's deflationary hoarding; Keynes's liquidity trap).

## The institutional layer — money is never studied in isolation

The stones give the **design space**; *which* design gets built where is an **institutional/power contest**, not a technical gradient (Quirk 18 — the state is inside the model). Extraction has **two axes**:

- **impersonal ↔ directed** — the impersonal skim (spreads; and the nominal-contract incidence of inflation, *transitional* rather than structural — Cantillon's own account ends in the injecting state's impoverishment, not a standing rent) vs the *aimed* lever (tariffs, devaluation, interest rates, sanctions): the **O-mode** wielding money as a weapon, **redistributive and punitive**. The reserve issuer taxes the whole world's holders; foreign holders have **zero recourse** — taxation without representation, coerced O-mode at global scale.
- **stabilising ↔ predatory** (the consent axis) — the *same* instruments are dual-use: shock-absorption (Krugman's stabiliser) *or* predation. The X-ray is *who bears it, did they consent, do they have recourse* — not "all policy is theft."

*Pedigree:* exorbitant privilege (Eichengreen), Triffin dilemma, beggar-thy-neighbour (Robinson), weaponised interdependence (Farrell & Newman), Hirschman's *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (no voice + locked-in exit = maximally extractable), financial repression (Reinhart, McKinnon–Shaw), institutional economics (North, Acemoglu–Robinson, Ostrom).

**Why the schools live at the macro lever-panel:** that's where extraction is *leveraged*, and where prestige and funding sit (public choice, turned on the profession). The macro aggregate (M2, CPI) is also a **veil** — it washes out *who bears the skim*. All three rival schools fetishise *controlling the aggregate* — goldbug *fixes* it, cryptopoof *caps* it, Keynesian *manages* it — fighting over the level of a number. The micro-ledger truth spoils all three at once: the action is in **relations and distribution**, not the total's level. The framework is **micro-first and arms the extracted** (Doña Elena; personal indices over CPI — Quirk 13). *A tool for the taxed, not the taxer.*

*(Calibration: Keynesians are not essentialists and do have microfoundations; the honest charge is that representative-agent macro washes out the distributional reality — HANK only lately restores it. Common thread: aggregate-veil + playbook-protection, not an identical error.)*

## The arc — money's roundabout regression

**Guard (canon: not predicting the future of money):** what follows is **forces + a comparative law**, never an outcome — no winner, no date. And it is *our school's* reading; a rival reads the same history bending toward its pole.

- **Origin** (Stones 1–2): a minimal ledger, credit-first, near-zero extraction.
- **The detour** (where it went): two *separable* trends — the **substrate thinned** (efficiency climbed the tradeability gradient, per `MONEY-ABIOGENESIS.md`) *while* **extraction accreted** in the sufficiency-gaps and was then weaponised geopolitically. Money grew more capable *and* more skimmed at once.
- **The contested field** (where it goes): **no single technical terminus.** The efficiency-pull points at a minimal/melting/unbundled/transparent ledger that *resembles the origin at higher technology* — the "roundabout regression," a spiral landing on a **ledger, not a commodity** (inverse of Mises). But that is only **one of three engineered attractors**, selected by the power contest:
  - **Relational-minimal** (efficiency pull) — *ours*.
  - **Essentialist-hard-money** (the push) — fixed/capped/appreciating.
  - **Authoritarian-control** (the O-mode) — surveilled, centralised, programmable, *extraction-as-feature* (China's e-CNY).
  This is **not a redemption arc**: the authoritarian pole means the arc can bend toward *more* extraction. No guaranteed ending.

**The comparative law (the real falsifiable claim):** money regresses toward the minimal ledger **where the extraction surface is contestable** (measured *ex-ante*: exit, transparency, competition) and **persists or is captured where it's defended**. This is **conditional beta-convergence** (Barro–Sala-i-Martin) applied to the ledger; contestability is itself the *prize* of the contest (China removes exit; weaponisation breeds exit-seeking — self-undermining, but slowly).

> **The three attractors are a *map* of the design-space poles — a taxonomy for reading where a money sits — not a test.** With all three currently observable, "one pole won locally" is unfalsifiable, and "my pole wins globally" is the forbidden prophecy. Only the **conditional law** has falsifiable content. The map labels the poles; the law is the physics.

**The efficiency↔extraction coupling (borrowed; cf. seigniorage / rent-extraction / Cantillon / financial-repression literature):** the properties that make money efficient are tied to its extractability. Our packaging: extraction lodges in the **axiom sufficiency-gaps**, so gap-closing strips the per-unit **rate** — *but* more-useful money grows the skim **base**. So "regression toward minimal = de-extraction" holds on the *rate* margin, not unconditionally; net extraction turns on which margin dominates, which contestability governs.

**Use:** a **lens, not a prophecy** — point it at any money and read which attractor pulls it, which surfaces are stripped vs dug-in, and whether the relevant surface is contestable or captured.
