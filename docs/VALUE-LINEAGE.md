# The Value Lineage — money's worth is floorless but fruitful

The framework's claim about _where money's value comes from_, with its honest pedigree. One line:

> **Money is valued because it produces the marginal trades that only exist once it's accepted — value with no floor underneath it and real cargo inside it.**

No objective anchor (no backing, no commodity, no intrinsic worth), and yet not arbitrary: the value is the surplus from trades that wouldn't have happened otherwise. Floorless _and_ fruitful.

## Three positions, not two

The usual debate is floor-vs-bubble. There's a third, and it's ours.

|                                             | grounded in?                                        | arbitrary?                                   | held object?                 |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Floor** (Mises, metallism, labour theory) | a prior substance / backing                         | no — but the grounding is a fiction          | yes                          |
| **Pure bubble** (Samuelson, Tirole)         | nothing                                             | **yes** — value for no reason; could be zero | yes (a held bubble-token)    |
| **Floorless-but-fruitful** (the framework)  | nothing _prior_ — the loop _produces_ a real payoff | **no** — the surplus is real                 | **no** — a net-zero relation |

The crux is the word **prior**. A _floor_ is value that exists _before and independent of_ acceptance (gold is valuable whether or not it's money). The _marginal-trades_ value exists _only because_ money is accepted — the trades clear only when others take it. So it sits **inside** the loop (the payoff the loop generates), not **under** it (a foundation). That's why it's not a floor, and why "we ground money in the trades it enables" does **not** smuggle objectivism back in.

## The lineage, sorted by what each one actually gives

**Georg Simmel — _The Philosophy of Money_ (1900). The philosophical backbone.**
Simmel didn't just say money is floorless; he said _all value_ is. Three moves that _are_ this theory:

- **Value is relational, not in objects** — _"value is not a property of things but a function of how we relate to them."_ No floor for **anything**. The special pleading dissolves at the root: it was never "everything subjective except money."
- **Exchange makes value _super-individual_** — private subjective valuations become a shared, coordinated, quasi-objective value through the exchange relation. _That is the common-knowledge / selection layer, named in 1900._
- **Value = resistance / distance / sacrifice overcome** — _"the more we have to wait, sacrifice, or strive for an object, the more we value it."_ This _is_ the transaction-cost theory, grounded: value is the _distance_ between desire and attainment; money's job is to shrink that distance; **money's value is the sum of distances it lets you bridge** — i.e. the marginal trades. Simmel tells you _why_ transaction-cost reduction is value: because value just _is_ the overcoming of resistance.

So Simmel already resolved floor-vs-bubble: value is **neither substance (floor) nor arbitrary (bubble)** — it's _distance-overcome-in-exchange_, floorless and fruitful, with money as the **purest case**, not the exception.

**Don Patinkin — _Money, Interest, and Prices_ (1956). The formal anti-regression move.**
Patinkin valued money by the **marginal utility of _holding_ real balances** (the liquidity/transaction service) and _dismissed_ the circularity charge — against Mises, who _"eluded the circularity by his regression theorem."_ Patinkin denied there was a circle to elude: value the _service of holding_, marginally, and integrate money into **general** value theory so it needs no separate, floored account. _Honest gap:_ he **posits** that holding yields utility (the "money-in-the-utility-function" shortcut) — he assumes the very thing to be explained. That gap is filled below.

**Lagos–Wright / Kiyotaki–Wright (New Monetarism). The microfoundation.**
They **derive** the liquidity service from explicit frictions (no double coincidence, no commitment, no memory, anonymity) — turning Patinkin's _posited_ utility-of-holding into a _derived_ result. Money is valued for the trades it unlocks under friction, priced at the margin (the carry-cost margin / convenience yield). This is Simmel's "distance overcome," formalized.

**Ostroy (1973) / Kocherlakota (1998). The mechanism, and the minimal form.**
Money is a _record-keeping device_ (Ostroy) — _equivalent to memory_ (Kocherlakota). This gives the **minimal form**: the net-zero **ledger**, no token at all (more minimal than a bubble-token). **Caveat:** this is _value-agnostic_ — record-keeping is a claim about _mechanism_, not value-source. It is compatible with objectivism. It does **no** anti-objectivist work; it just tells you what money _is_ (a thin record). The subjectivism comes from Simmel/Patinkin, not from here.

**Ludwig von Mises — the special-pleader (refused).**
The regression theorem: ground money's value by regressing through time to a prior **commodity** use-value. This carves money out as the one good needing an objective anchor — special pleading inconsistent with thoroughgoing subjectivism (the charge Patinkin made explicit). We do not build it. _There is no `Mises` contract, on purpose._

**Carl Menger — the flinching forerunner.**
Subjectivized value (the marginal revolution, the diamond-water resolution) — but kept the **commodity origin** for money (the symmetry-breaker Mises later hardened into the regression). A partial ally: right about value, flinched on money. "More Mengerian than Menger" was the early slogan; the sharper statement is **"Simmel without the substance-nostalgia."**

**Samuelson (1958) / Tirole (1985) — no-backing, but not minimal and not non-arbitrary.**
The overlapping-generations / rational-bubble result: fiat money is a **pure bubble** — a self-fulfilling fixed point with _no fundamental_, sustained by an infinite forward relay. This proves **floorless value is a consistent equilibrium** (the formal "look ma, no hands"; it dissolves Mises's circularity by showing the circle is a _fixed point_, not vicious). But:

- it's **not minimal** — their money is still a _held bubble-token_ (a positive-valued store-object), carrying the store-of-value bolt-on;
- it's **arbitrary** — it proves value _can_ exist (multiple equilibria, the worthless one always available) but not _why it does_ (it punts on **selection**);
- it leans on an **infinite horizon** to avoid backward-induction unraveling.

## The resolutions this lineage produces

- **Inside-the-loop payoff, not under-the-loop floor.** The marginal trades are _generated by_ acceptance, not _prior to_ it. Floorless and real.
- **No-backing ≠ no-substance.** Samuelson/Tirole removed the _backing_ (floor) but kept the _substance-form_ (a held token). Simmel + the ledger remove the substance too: value as pure relation, money as a net-zero record. _Minimal_ is the stronger claim than _unbacked_.
- **The bootstrap is legitimate but incomplete.** A self-fulfilling fixed point is a real object (not a vicious circle). But it proves _consistency_, not _selection_, and parks on an infinite horizon. The framework supplies the **selection** (common knowledge of acceptance — Simmel's "super-individual") and **shortens the loop** (mutual credit, settled-as-you-go, clears/jubilees — far less dependent on an infinite relay of future strangers than a held bubble-token).
- **The special pleading dissolves by _integration_.** Simmel (all value relational, money the exemplar) and Patinkin (money valued by the same marginal utility, in the same general equilibrium, as everything) both refuse to give money its own theory of value. Money's floorlessness is just _value's_ floorlessness, made visible — because money is the one good that is _nothing but_ the relation.

## The image: a sea anchor, not a floor anchor

A _floor anchor_ fixes you to the seabed (objective value, the bottom turtle). A _sea anchor_ drags against the **water itself** — it stabilises you relative to the _medium_ (the network of expectations) while holding you to nothing fixed. Money's steadiness is drag against a vast, slow medium, not grounding in a substance. And the drag is **productive**: it's the friction that becomes the marginal trades. So — _"look ma, no hands, and it's carrying cargo."_ No seabed beneath it; a hold full of real trades it alone made possible. The ledger goes one better: it isn't even an object bobbing in the water — it's the _pattern of the current_, summing to zero. Nothing to sink.

## What the framework adds

Not the value theory (Simmel had it; Patinkin formalised the no-regression move; Lagos–Wright microfounded the service). The framework's own contributions are:

1. **The synthesis** — Simmel (philosophy) + Patinkin (formal) + Lagos–Wright (frictions) + Ostroy/Kocherlakota (the minimal ledger form), assembled and made consistent.
2. **The selection story made operational** — common knowledge of acceptance as _why_ the monetary equilibrium obtains (the part OLG punts).
3. **The minimal mutual-credit form** — net-zero, no token, settled-as-you-go: the least bubble-dependent, most minimal realisation (`Kocherlakota.sol`, the two-line transfer at the core).

## Where it lives in the code

At the heart of `contracts/Kocherlakota.sol`, in `pay`:

```solidity
// the whole of money, here: one mark down, one mark up, on a record we both
// trust, summing to zero. everything else in this repository is the calculus.
balance[msg.sender] = newPayerBalance;   // one mark down
balance[to]        += int256(amount);    // one mark up
```

Net-zero, floorless, fruitful: a record that produces the trades, grounded in nothing prior, summing to zero. The doing is stone-age; the proof — this document and that repository — is the calculus we were late to write.
