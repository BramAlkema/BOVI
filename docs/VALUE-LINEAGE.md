# The Value Lineage — money's worth is floorless but fruitful

The framework's claim about *where money's value comes from*, with its honest pedigree. One line:

> **Money is valued because it produces the marginal trades that only exist once it's accepted — value with no floor underneath it and real cargo inside it.**

No objective anchor (no backing, no commodity, no intrinsic worth), and yet not arbitrary: the value is the surplus from trades that wouldn't have happened otherwise. Floorless *and* fruitful.

## Three positions, not two

The usual debate is floor-vs-bubble. There's a third, and it's ours.

| | grounded in? | arbitrary? | held object? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Floor** (Mises, metallism, labour theory) | a prior substance / backing | no — but the grounding is a fiction | yes |
| **Pure bubble** (Samuelson, Tirole) | nothing | **yes** — value for no reason; could be zero | yes (a held bubble-token) |
| **Floorless-but-fruitful** (the framework) | nothing *prior* — the loop *produces* a real payoff | **no** — the surplus is real | **no** — a net-zero relation |

The crux is the word **prior**. A *floor* is value that exists *before and independent of* acceptance (gold is valuable whether or not it's money). The *marginal-trades* value exists *only because* money is accepted — the trades clear only when others take it. So it sits **inside** the loop (the payoff the loop generates), not **under** it (a foundation). That's why it's not a floor, and why "we ground money in the trades it enables" does **not** smuggle objectivism back in.

## The lineage, sorted by what each one actually gives

**Georg Simmel — *The Philosophy of Money* (1900). The philosophical backbone.**
Simmel didn't just say money is floorless; he said *all value* is. Three moves that *are* this theory:
- **Value is relational, not in objects.** In his words (Ch. 1 §I): value is *"in no case a 'property'"* of things — *"eine »Eigenschaft« ihrer ist der Wert in keinem Fall"* — and, of the economic case, *"economic value is not an inherent quality of an object, but is established by the expenditure of another object which is given in exchange for it."* No floor for **anything**. The special pleading dissolves at the root: it was never "everything subjective except money." *(Simmel's next move is important and ours: he immediately refuses to leave value in the subject either — the relation is where it lands.)*
- **Exchange makes value *supra-individual*** — and this is the sentence to quote, because it states floorless-but-fruitful with the anti-objectivist hedge already in it: *"In exchange, value becomes supra-subjective, supra-individual, yet without becoming an objective quality and reality of the things themselves."* Private valuations become shared and coordinated without ever becoming a property of the stuff. *That is the common-knowledge / selection layer, named in 1900.*
- **Value = resistance / distance / sacrifice overcome** — *"Objects are not difficult to acquire because they are valuable, but we call those objects valuable that resist our desire to possess them"*; value arises from *"the conquest of distance, obstacles and difficulties."* This *is* the transaction-cost theory, grounded: value is the *distance* between desire and attainment; money's job is to shrink that distance; **money's value is the sum of distances it lets you bridge** — i.e. the marginal trades. Simmel tells you *why* transaction-cost reduction is value: because value just *is* the overcoming of resistance.

So Simmel already resolved floor-vs-bubble: value is **neither substance (floor) nor arbitrary (bubble)** — it's *distance-overcome-in-exchange*, floorless and fruitful, with money as the **purest case**, not the exception. He says that last part outright, as a programme: he sets out to develop money as value's *"Gipfel und reinsten Ausdruck"* — its summit and purest expression — and closes the argument by calling money *"the reification of the pure relationship between things as expressed in their economic motion."* One nuance worth keeping rather than smoothing: Simmel registers that money *appears* to escape relativity — *"by sublimating the relativity of things, money seems to avoid relativity"* — and then dissolves the appearance. That step is the answer to "but surely money is special," and it is his, not ours.

**Don Patinkin — *Money, Interest, and Prices* (1956). The formal anti-regression move.**
Patinkin valued money by the **marginal utility of *holding* real balances** (the liquidity/transaction service) and *dismissed* the circularity charge — against Mises, who *"eluded the circularity by his regression theorem."* Patinkin denied there was a circle to elude: value the *service of holding*, marginally, and integrate money into **general** value theory so it needs no separate, floored account. *Honest gap:* he **posits** that holding yields utility (the "money-in-the-utility-function" shortcut) — he assumes the very thing to be explained. That gap is filled below.

**Lagos–Wright / Kiyotaki–Wright (New Monetarism). The microfoundation.**
They **derive** the liquidity service from explicit frictions (no double coincidence, no commitment, no memory, anonymity) — turning Patinkin's *posited* utility-of-holding into a *derived* result. Money is valued for the trades it unlocks under friction, priced at the margin (the carry-cost margin / convenience yield). This is Simmel's "distance overcome," formalized.

**Choi & Rocheteau — *Money Mining and Price Dynamics* (2019). The direction, tested.**
The mining extension of the same family: money privately produced at a time-cost, with an occupation choice that makes the cost of supplying it endogenous. Its gift to this lineage is the causal-direction test, run on the commodity case: the **purchasing power of gold Granger-causes gold production** (1880–1970), and Bitcoin price growth Granger-causes mining difficulty. Value moves first; production chases it. Stated at exact strength: this is evidence against the *folk* cost story — worth flowing from the toil into the thing, supply first and value after — on that story's home ground. It does **not** refute equilibrium cost-of-production theories, which posit exactly this supply response (C&R credit Bordo 1981 for the idea); precedence evidence cannot adjudicate a two-way adjustment. The framework's declared target was always the folk physics, so the narrower claim is the right one — and the stronger for being the one the data can carry. *Honest limits:* a working paper (cite the direction, not the coefficients), and its bootstrap-side results — the holding return as the wage that calls supply into existence — live in the cold-start and bootstrap-engine docs, where they now do their work.

**Ostroy (1973) / Kocherlakota (1998). The mechanism, and the minimal form.**
Money is a *record-keeping device* (Ostroy) — *a primitive form of memory* (Kocherlakota's own phrase; his theorem is an inclusion, not an equivalence — anything money achieves, memory achieves, and the converse fails in stranger-matching, where memory strictly dominates). This gives the **minimal form**: the net-zero **ledger**, no token at all (more minimal than a bubble-token — though note the token's floor at zero is a real feature the signed ledger gives up: it is the enforcement). **Caveat:** this is *value-agnostic* — record-keeping is a claim about *mechanism*, not value-source. It is compatible with objectivism. It does **no** anti-objectivist work; it just tells you what money *is* (a thin record). The subjectivism comes from Simmel/Patinkin, not from here.

**Ludwig von Mises — the special-pleader (refused).**
The regression theorem: ground money's value by regressing through time to a prior **commodity** use-value. This carves money out as the one good needing an objective anchor — special pleading inconsistent with thoroughgoing subjectivism (the charge Patinkin made explicit). We do not build it. *There is no `Mises` contract, on purpose.*

**Carl Menger — the flinching forerunner.**
Subjectivized value (the marginal revolution, the diamond-water resolution) — but kept the **commodity origin** for money (the symmetry-breaker Mises later hardened into the regression). A partial ally: right about value, flinched on money. "More Mengerian than Menger" was the early slogan; the sharper statement is **"Simmel without the substance-nostalgia."**

**Samuelson (1958) / Tirole (1985) — the contrast case, and *not* this framework's proof.**
The overlapping-generations / rational-bubble result: an intrinsically worthless durable token holds positive value forever as a self-fulfilling fixed point with *no fundamental*, sustained by an endless relay of new entrants. It dissolves Mises's circularity by showing the circle is a *fixed point*, not vicious — a real and permanent gain. But audited (2026-08) against the primary texts, it does **not** prove what this document long claimed it proved, for four reasons:

- **Tirole forbids our combination — this is the decisive one.** His §6 dichotomy: *"Either it is essentially depicted as a store of value (bubble) as in Samuelson, or it is assumed to serve transaction purposes. The two representations are in the long run inconsistent."* Proposition 7: under a non-vanishing transaction role, *"there cannot exist a bubble on money."* So "pure bubble **plus** a real liquidity service" is precisely the pair he rules out. **Floorless-but-fruitful is not a bubble theory and never was** — it is Tirole's *fundamentalist* view, where money's whole value is the discounted stream of services acceptance makes possible. Floorless because nothing precedes acceptance; fruitful because the services are real. That position is coherent, it is ours, and the bubble is its **contrast**, not its proof.
- **The object is wrong for us.** OLG money is a life-cycle store of value that never mediates a trade — Samuelson: *"With ideal clearing arrangements, money as a medium of exchange might have little function"*; McCallum (1983): the framework *"neglects the medium-of-exchange property that is the distinguishing characteristic of money."* Our money is a transaction rail. **The consistency proof for *our* object is Kiyotaki–Wright / Lagos–Wright above** — also intrinsically worthless, also self-fulfilling, but with the transaction role modelled rather than assumed away.
- **It carries an unstated empirical precondition.** Tirole's Prop. 1: bubbles exist only when the interest rate is at or below the growth rate (dynamic inefficiency); above it, *"bubbles are ruled out by wealth constraints."* Whether advanced economies satisfy this is live and disputed (Abel–Mankiw–Summers–Zeckhauser say efficient; Geerolf disputes the measurement; Blanchard 2019 on safe rates below growth). Santos–Woodford (1997) bound it further. And Prop. 1(c) itself now has a published correction (Pham & Toda, *Econometrica* 2026).
- **The infinite horizon is load-bearing, not technical** — and Samuelson conceded it himself (fn. 19): with a known terminal generation the value unravels backward, and *"perfect competition shrugs its shoulders at such a question."*

*Two corrections to how this document used to describe them.* "Arbitrary — value for no reason" was mis-stated: what is unpinned is the price level and which of a continuum of equilibria obtains, while the monetary equilibrium is *selected* by Pareto efficiency (it is the one that reaches the golden rule). And "they kept the substance-form, a held token" is blunter than we claimed — Samuelson's fn. 21 already allows *"the new durable moneys **(or records)**"*, so the ledger reading was anticipated; what remains distinctive is the net-zero form, not the record as such.

## The resolutions this lineage produces

- **Inside-the-loop payoff, not under-the-loop floor.** The marginal trades are *generated by* acceptance, not *prior to* it. Floorless and real.
- **No-backing ≠ no-substance.** Samuelson/Tirole removed the *backing* (floor) but kept a positive-valued held object. Simmel + the ledger remove that too: value as pure relation, money as a **net-zero** record. *Minimal* is the stronger claim than *unbacked* — and net-zero, not record-versus-token, is where the distance actually lies (Samuelson's fn. 21 already said "moneys (or records)").
- **The bootstrap is legitimate but incomplete.** A self-fulfilling fixed point is a real object (not a vicious circle). But it proves *consistency*, not *selection*, and parks on an infinite horizon. The framework supplies the **selection** (common knowledge of acceptance — Simmel's *überindividuell*, "supra-individual") and **shortens the loop** (mutual credit, settled-as-you-go, clears/jubilees — far less dependent on an endless relay of future strangers). Note the honest half-credit owed here: in the OLG setting the monetary equilibrium *is* picked out, by Pareto efficiency; what selection leaves open is the price level and the choice among a continuum, not whether value can be had for no reason at all.
- **The special pleading dissolves by *integration*.** Simmel (all value relational, money the exemplar) and Patinkin (money valued by the same marginal utility, in the same general equilibrium, as everything) both refuse to give money its own theory of value. Money's floorlessness is just *value's* floorlessness, made visible — because money is the one good that is *nothing but* the relation.

## The image: a sea anchor, not a floor anchor

A *floor anchor* fixes you to the seabed (objective value, the bottom turtle). A *sea anchor* drags against the **water itself** — it stabilises you relative to the *medium* (the network of expectations) while holding you to nothing fixed. Money's steadiness is drag against a vast, slow medium, not grounding in a substance. And the drag is **productive**: it's the friction that becomes the marginal trades. So — *"look ma, no hands, and it's carrying cargo."* No seabed beneath it; a hold full of real trades it alone made possible. The ledger goes one better: it isn't even an object bobbing in the water — it's the *pattern of the current*, summing to zero. Nothing to sink.

## What the framework adds

Not the value theory (Simmel had it; Patinkin formalised the no-regression move; Lagos–Wright microfounded the service). The framework's own contributions are:
1. **The synthesis** — Simmel (philosophy) + Patinkin (formal) + Lagos–Wright (frictions) + Ostroy/Kocherlakota (the minimal ledger form), assembled and made consistent.
2. **The selection story made operational** — common knowledge of acceptance as *why* the monetary equilibrium obtains (the part OLG punts).
3. **The minimal mutual-credit form** — net-zero, no token, settled-as-you-go: the least bubble-dependent, most minimal realisation (`Kocherlakota.sol`, the two-line transfer at the core).

## Where it lives in the code

At the heart of `contracts/Kocherlakota.sol`, in `pay`:

```solidity
// the whole of money, here: one mark down, one mark up, on a record we both
// trust, summing to zero. everything else in this repository is the calculus.
balance[msg.sender] = newPayerBalance;   // one mark down
balance[to]        += int256(amount);    // one mark up
```

Net-zero, floorless, fruitful: a record that produces the trades, grounded in nothing prior, summing to zero. The doing is stone-age; the proof — this document and that repository — is the calculus we were late to write.
