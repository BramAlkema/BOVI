# The Island of Minimal Money: A BOVI Educational Storyboard

> **Reconciliation header — predates `docs/FOUNDATIONS.md`; read with this correction.** This is an early pedagogical draft and carries the framing the rebuilt foundation corrects. In Episode 1 it **headlines barter** as money's failed first step (*"The Failed Solution: Barter"*), lands on **shells/tokens** rather than **credit/a record first**, and uses *"forgetful ledger"* to deny memory; later episodes have the four **modes "emerge"** out of Value-money. The rebuilt view (Stones 1–2): money is pulled by the need for a **record of who-gave-and-didn't-receive** — that's **credit / a ledger from the start** (a *token* is just a later, optional substrate); the **barter / double-coincidence case is the acute *Value-mode* slice, not the origin**; and the four modes are **co-present from the start**, not spawned by Value. The corrected Episode 1 is the **axe-spear story** (lands on the tally, no barter-myth headline, floorless-but-fruitful). Keep this as a draft; teach from the corrected versions.

## Setting
**12 people shipwrecked on an island with diverse skills:**
- Alice (farmer)
- Bob (builder) 
- Carol (coder)
- Dan (doctor)
- Eve (engineer)
- Frank (fisherman)
- Grace (gatherer)
- Henry (hunter)
- Iris (inventor)
- Jack (jeweler)
- Kate (keeper of records)
- Luis (lumberjack)

They need to build a functioning economy from scratch.

## Episode 1: "The Portable Forgetful Ledger"
*Day 1-7: The birth of minimal money*

### The Problem
- Alice has coconuts, needs shelter
- Bob can build, needs food
- Frank has fish, needs tools
- **Nobody has what the other needs when they need it**

### The Failed Solution: Barter
"I'll trade you 3 fish for 2 coconuts... but I actually need wood..."
*Triple coincidence of wants is impossible*

### The Breakthrough
**Carol suggests**: "What if we used these unique shells as tokens?"
- There are exactly 1,200 shells on the beach (verifiable)
- Each person gets 100 shells to start (symmetric knowledge)
- Shells can be broken for smaller denominations (divisible)
- Everyone knows a real shell from a fake (verifiable)

### The Success
- Alice sells coconuts for shells
- Uses shells to buy shelter from Bob
- Bob uses shells to buy fish from Frank
- **Trades that were impossible now happen!**

### The Realization
Kate observes: "The shells don't remember who had them or why. They're like a **portable forgetful ledger** - they only track who has what NOW, not the history."

**LESSON**: Minimal money is just transferable tokens with known supply that unlock blocked trades.

## Episode 2: "The Price Memory Crisis"
*Day 8-30: The invention of PDAs*

### The Problem
Wild price fluctuations:
- Monday: 1 fish = 2 shells
- Tuesday: 1 fish = 5 shells (storm, scarce fish)
- Wednesday: 1 fish = 1 shell (huge catch)

**Dan**: "I can't remember what anything costs! Was 10 shells for medicine expensive or cheap?"

### The Failed Solution: Mental Tracking
Everyone tries to remember all prices. Chaos ensues:
- Arguments about "fair" prices
- Nobody can compare values across time
- Trade slows down from confusion

### The Breakthrough
**Carol creates the first PDA** (Price Discovery Assistant):
```
Fish Price History:
- 7 days ago: 2 shells
- 6 days ago: 2 shells  
- 5 days ago: 5 shells (storm)
- Today: 1 shell
- Average: 2.5 shells
- Trend: Stabilizing
```

### The Success
- Everyone gets a "price book" (primitive PDA)
- Can see if current prices are high/low
- Can track their spending patterns
- Trade confidence returns

### The Realization
**Iris notes**: "The shells don't need to be stable or remember prices. That's the PDA's job! Money just needs to enable trades."

**LESSON**: Unit of account is separate from medium of exchange. PDAs handle price memory.

---

## Episode 3: "The House Contract Disaster"
*Day 31-60: The creation of indices*

### The Problem
Bob wants to build Eve a house:
- **Contract**: "Pay me 500 shells in 6 months"
- **Month 1**: Hurricane creates shell demand, now worth double
- **Month 3**: Calm period, shells worth half
- **Month 6**: Eve owes 500 shells but that's now worth 10x the original labor!

**Eve**: "This is insane! The house value didn't change but my debt is crushing!"

### The Failed Solution: Renegotiation
Every long-term contract becomes a fight:
- Constant renegotiation
- Nobody wants to commit long-term
- Construction stops, planning impossible

### The Breakthrough
**Kate invents the first index**:
"Let's track a basket of real goods:"
```
Island Living Index (ILI):
- 10 fish (protein)
- 20 coconuts (calories)
- 5 lumber pieces (materials)
- 2 tool repairs (services)
= 1 ILI Unit
```

**New contracts**: "Pay me 5 ILI units in 6 months (paid in shells at future ILI rate)"

### The Success
- Long-term contracts become possible
- House building resumes
- Medical service subscriptions emerge
- Future planning returns

### The Realization
**Henry observes**: "We're separating the contract layer from the transaction layer. The index provides stability, shells just settle the final payment."

**LESSON**: Long-term contracts need stable indices separate from fluctuating money.

## Episode 4: "The Idle Shell Problem"
*Day 61-90: The birth of account sweeping*

### The Problem
Grace is saving shells for winter:
- Keeps 300 shells buried (25% of all shells!)
- Meanwhile, Frank needs shells to buy new nets
- Iris needs shells to fund tool innovation
- **Idle shells create artificial scarcity**

### The Failed Solution: Shell Hoarding
Others start hoarding too:
- 60% of shells get buried/hidden
- Trade velocity crashes
- Economy grinds to halt
- Innovation stops from lack of funding

### The Breakthrough
**Carol creates the first "sweep contract"**:
```
Daily Shell Sweep System:
- End of day: Unused shells go to "Investment Pool"
- Pool funds productive projects (nets, tools, farms)
- Returns paid in goods/services
- Can withdraw for trades anytime
```

**Grace's new approach**:
- Evening: 290 shells swept to fund Frank's nets
- Morning: Withdraws 10 shells for daily trades
- Month end: Receives fish dividend from net profits

### The Success
- Shell velocity increases 5x
- Innovation gets funded
- Savers earn returns
- Traders have liquidity

### The Realization
**Jack notes**: "Holding transaction tokens is wasteful. Wealth should be in productive assets, shells should just flow through for trades."

**LESSON**: Store of value belongs in productive assets, not in idle money.

## Episode 5: "The Bundle Inevitably Returns"
*Day 91-120: All four fairness modes emerge*

### The Emerging Complications

**Balanced Mode Emerges** (Debt Tracking):
- Luis lends shells to Dan for medical supplies
- Kate starts tracking who owes whom
- "Forgetful" ledger gains memory through debt records
- Credit relationships form outside shell system

**Obligated Mode Emerges** (Hierarchy):
- Someone needs to maintain the shell supply (prevent counterfeiting)
- Island council forms, demands "tax" shells for public goods
- Lighthouse building, path maintenance need funding
- Authority structure develops around resource allocation

**Immediate Mode Emerges** (Gifts):
- Families don't want to charge each other shells
- Alice's child gets sick, community gives without tracking
- Gift economy emerges parallel to shell economy
- "Some things shouldn't be transactions"

**Value Mode Persists** (Market):
- Shells still enabling pure market trades
- But now embedded in wider social system
- Can't escape the other modes

### The Final Realization

**Carol's Observation**: 
"We started with minimal money - just portable tokens. But look what we've built around it:
- PDAs for price tracking (separate from money)
- Indices for contracts (separate from money)
- Sweeping for wealth storage (separate from money)
- Debt ledgers for credit (Balanced mode)
- Taxes for public goods (Obligated mode)
- Gift culture for social bonds (Immediate mode)

**We unbundled the functions but couldn't escape the modes!**"

### The Ultimate Lesson

**Kate's Summary**: 
"Every society needs:
1. Spot trades (Value mode with minimal money)
2. Time-shifted obligations (Balanced mode with debt tracking)
3. Collective resources (Obligated mode with taxation)
4. Social cohesion (Immediate mode with gifts)

You can pretend one money does all this (bundling), or explicitly separate the tools (unbundling), but you cannot escape needing all four modes."

## Assessment Questions After Each Episode

**Episode 1**: "Why did shells work as money even though they're worthless?"
*Answer: Symmetric knowledge of supply, not intrinsic value*

**Episode 2**: "Why did the PDA solve the price chaos?"
*Answer: Separated price memory from exchange medium*

**Episode 3**: "What's wrong with denominating long-term contracts in fluctuating money?"
*Answer: Creates unfair risk that has nothing to do with the actual exchange*

**Episode 4**: "Why is holding transaction money wasteful?"
*Answer: Opportunity cost - wealth should generate returns*

**Episode 5**: "Could the island maintain purely minimal money forever?"
*Answer: No - human exchange naturally involves multiple relationship types and fairness modes*
