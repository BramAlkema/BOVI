# The Four Modes
*An Anthropology of Exchange*

Working title for a 23-chapter book in nine parts on money, the four fairness modes, and how to live in an unbundled world.

## Voice and structural decisions

**Voice — "we".** Inclusive, observational, collegial. Author and reader on the same side, observing the framework together. Avoids both the prescriptive "you" and the formal "one." The "we" never lectures — it mostly says *"we see,"* *"we look for,"* *"we encounter."*

**The one exception — the Preface is "I", and this is settled.** Front matter is the author speaking as author, before the collegial voice starts: the anomaly is one person's, the calibration is one person's, and the admission about what a contract cannot prove has to be signed by somebody. Do not normalise it to "we" on a later pass. The switch is performed, not merely stated — the Preface's only "we" is its final sentence, which hands the voice over: *"From here on the voice is we, because from here on we are looking at the same thing."* That sentence is load-bearing and should survive editing.

**Opening flip.** Doña Elena (Buenos Aires) is the protagonist of the framework. The Berlin barista is the puzzle to be solved. The book teaches *from* the global-majority experience *toward* the rich-country case, not the reverse. This commits the book on page 1 to the substantivist anthropology stance.

**Vignettes inside chapters, not as Part-openers.** Scenes serve the analytical work of each chapter, not a separate narrative arc. Sam-as-protagonist dissolves; Doña Elena, Mateo, Lucia, the bridal party become recurring witnesses examined under different analytical lenses.

## Parts and chapters

### Front matter

*The author's entry, before the "we" voice starts. The origin question stated as an anomaly rather than an indictment — the field holds subjective valuation, teaches it in the demand chapter, and switches ontology at money without marking the switch — with the evidence for that held at its actual strength (middle level piloted, the within-book switch not yet established). Then the practical question the book answers: can the introductory account be taught without the switch? Posture: efficacy over novelty, everything borrowed. Closes by separating **constructive** rigour (a mechanism written as an executable contract cannot gesture at a step) from **empirical** testability (the corpus study, the artifact program) — because conflating them is the first available overclaim. Hands off to Ch 1 rather than summarising it.*

### Part II — Money as Coordination Infrastructure
*Money is a sufficiently-reliable ledger of who has given and not yet received; its value is floorless but fruitful — the trades it unlocks, pulled from the future, not pushed from the past. Common knowledge of acceptance **selects** which money wins; it does not create the worth. And every money requires a bootstrap. The joint with Part I lands here (Ch 4): a mode is a rule for when and whether the ledger must clear, and money is the Value discipline given infrastructure. Floorless is not arbitrary — the worth is estimable and stable, and every term in it is conditional on other people, which is a fixed point rather than an anchor. And removing the floor incurs a debt: Ch 5 leaves the reader asking **then what stops them?**, and the rest of the book is the working.*

## Cross-book subarcs

These run underneath the chapter structure and reward the careful reader:

1. **The Doña Elena revisitation** — five appearances (Chs 1, 7, 14, 19, 20), each examining the same scene under a different analytical lens.
2. **The bridal party fight** — four appearances (Chs 2, 12, 15, 22): raw confusion → Balanced-mode pathology → mode-tagged dissolution → the tool that would have helped.
3. **The 3× credit card** — four appearances (Chs 4, 11, 16, and once more in Ch 21, where she is the one keeping the notebook): friction as diagnostic → Value-mode definition → adversarial mode forcing. (€5 → €16: the official-rate fiction ≈3× plus DCC, spread, and fee — reconstructible arithmetic, and the mechanism seeds the cueva of Ch 13.)
4. **Bitcoin re-evaluation** — appears critically in early chapters, sympathetically in Ch 6 (cold-start), settling at "bundled at bootstrap, instrumental at use."
5. **The Berlin barista** — bookends the volume (Ch 1 as puzzle, Ch 23 as completion). She is never named.
6. **The defensive vocabulary** — accumulated chapter by chapter; collected in Ch 23. The reader notices it has been built only at the end.
7. **The clearing-discipline arc** — a mode is a rule for when/whether the ledger must clear; money is the Value discipline given infrastructure. Seeded in Ch 2 (Fiske's four scale types — the modes count differently), stated in Ch 4, then: engines as mode-subsidies (Ch 6), thinness as discipline-choice (8A), the four mechanisms at societal scale (Ch 10), mode-blind tools as forced clearing (Chs 12, 16), collapse as fallback down the discipline stack (Ch 19), the Tally as the four-discipline ledger restored (Ch 22), credited to Fiske (22A), formalised (Epilogue A.9). This is the joint between the modes half and the money half of the book.
8. **The push/pull arc — the book's hill.** The heresy 99% of the world rejects, delivered intuitively, not just formally: worth is pulled from the margin (the next trade), never pushed from behind. Installed in Ch 5 as three deckchair tests (the backing dial, 1971 both ways; the Zimbabwe note whose past is intact and worth is gone; the order a currency dies — savings → contracts → bread, the longest pull snaps first) + the push/pull vocabulary named. Lived in 8A (the rope), confirmed as autopsy in Ch 19 (value drains from the far future toward the present), weaponised in Ch 22 (the balance-as-silo interface is push's daily catechism; the Tally shows a *position*, pull-native), installed as the question beneath the six in Ch 23 and the Field Book, proven formally in Epilogue A.4/A.10 (the valuation recursion runs t → t+1; Mises runs t → t−1).
9. **The money-exception arc — the pedagogy underneath the error.** Introductory economics makes ordinary value subjective, then quietly changes grammar when money arrives: commodities *have* value, money *stores* it, prices *measure* it, backing or law *gives* it. Seeded in Ch 1 as the textbook mistaking a successful interface for an ontology; exposed in Ch 4 as object-versus-ledger; named in Ch 5 as monetary exceptionalism; paid off in Ch 6 by the goldbug as Econ-101 literalist and in Ch 7 by the forced functions bundle; made visible rather than argued in 8A's rope; rehearsed daily by the balance-as-silo interface in Ch 22; traced to a discipline that knew the correction only in pieces in 22A; and broken permanently by Ch 23's direction question. No professor has to believe the whole picture for the syllabus to install it.

10. **The governance debt — who holds the pen.** Removing the floor takes away what the reader was holding, and anti-essentialism with no discipline attached reads as *permission*: money is arbitrary, so the powerful may do as they like — the goldbug's fear, confirmed in the goldbug's own words. The arc pays the debt. Backing let people audit a *thing*; pull makes us watch *people*; the substance never removed the need to watch, it gave everyone permission to look away. Incurred and named in Ch 5 (and deliberately left unpaid), the demand conceded and the label refused in Ch 6, made concrete as *who sets the clearing rule, and in whose favour* in Chs 13 and 17 — where the arc meets its hardest case, because central bank independence is the book's own prescription implemented at continental scale, and the pen was taken from someone *accountable* and handed to someone *credible* — paid off in Ch 19 as the mirror of collapse (a loose anchor lets value flee; a rigid one makes people flee), read as conduct failure rather than nominal event across Chs 18–20, brought back to the reader's own scale in Ch 21 — where the arc admits its range, and the direction that governs it is stated (seeing points upward at the pen, arming points inward at your own arrangements) — then armed in Chs 22–23 with the asymmetry that keeps it from becoming surveillance — **visibility aimed at whoever holds the pen, never at whoever holds the balance** — and formalised in the Epilogue, where the machinery names every point at which it hands back to a person, and admits that nothing in it can act until someone calls it.

## Editorial notes

- **Audience**: trade non-fiction with academic credibility. Aiming for *Atlantic* and *Cambridge Journal of Economics* both — knowing that's structurally difficult.
- **Length target**: ~80,000 words (~3,500 per chapter average; some shorter, the dense ones longer).
- **Anthropology lineage** (Mauss → Polanyi → Sahlins → Fiske → Graeber → Maurer) lives mostly in footnotes and the closing bibliography. Not foregrounded in body text.
- **No mathematics in the body.** Teaching artifacts (tally rope, Doña Elena's iPhone, the Big Mac War) carry the conceptual load. The optional Machinery section carries the formal results and executable canon; supply-demand graphs and indifference curves still go in other books.
