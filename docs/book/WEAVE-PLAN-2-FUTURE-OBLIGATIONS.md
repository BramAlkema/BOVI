## P1 — the book is wrong or incomplete without these

### Ch 5 · the specimen correction — Lima yields to the Swiss dinar
**Where:** the passage in `### The thing it never needed` that calls the Lima dollar "the clean refutation of the naïve story that money is valuable only because a state demands it for tax."
**Why:** the session's adversarial pass broke that argument — the foreign holder is *downstream of the sink through the trading network*; the tax's bid propagates to every connected node, so extraterritorial circulation refutes nothing by itself. A hostile reader who knows the reply reads the book's killer counterexample as a miss. The clean refutation is the specimen with the ingredient zeroed: the Iraqi Swiss dinar, 1993–2003 — issuer demonetized it, no state accepted it, no bank redeemed it, the supply frozen by missing printing plates, and it circulated in the Kurdish north for a decade.
**Beat:** keep Lima, demoted to what it actually shows — the network's reach. Then the dinar as the experiment: delete every obligation and the value survives; therefore the obligation was never the source. Truth-sandwich; the myth gets one flat clause. Close with the honest wrinkle, *after* the argument per the concession rule: the dinar's late rise tracked the odds of future re-adoption — expectation doing the work, which is the book's own claim arriving from the other side.
**Payoff:** the chapter's central refutation now survives the strongest known counter, and Ch 19 inherits a ready-made case.

### Ch 18 · Sargent's endings — the thesis, photographed
**Where:** inside the chapter's existing draft arc; the executor reads the chapter first and places it where the common-knowledge claim peaks.
**Why:** the chapter argues inflation is broken common knowledge. The strongest empirical fact in the literature is sitting unclaimed: the four interwar hyperinflations ended *abruptly* — and note circulation *kept growing rapidly* after each stabilization. Not tightening; believed regime change. There is no better evidence for this chapter's title anywhere.
**Beat:** four countries, one shape — the moment it became widely believed that the government would no longer be financed by the printing of money, the inflation stopped, while the quantity of money kept rising. Then the Rentenmark, one paragraph: its celebrated "backing" by a mortgage on German land was a fiction nobody could foreclose and almost nobody ever redeemed; the note was not even legal tender; the only compulsion anywhere in its design was that public pay-offices had to accept it, alongside a hard cap and a budget balanced within two months. The story about the land was a prop for common knowledge; the working parts were a bounded issuer and a door that had to open.
**Payoff:** the chapter's claim stops being an interpretation and becomes a description of the record. Bridge to Ch 19: stabilization is repair of belief, not repair of quantity.
**Care:** the minority reading (the exchange-rate peg as the operative mechanism) exists; one clause, after, not before.

### Ch 6 · the symmetric demotion, the size condition, and born pre-sold
**Where:** three insertions in the existing draft: the state-coercion engine under `### The four known engines`; `### The math is brutal`; and a short beat near the chapter's close.
**Why:** the chapter already performs the demotion on the chartalist half — "Tax answers *why this money gets its first users*, not *what those users ultimately find worth having*" — a sentence that survived the session's adversarial pass without amendment. It owes the same demotion to the other side, and it is missing the engine's size condition and the case that has no cold start at all.
**Beats:**
- *The symmetry.* The oldest answer to money's value — trace acceptance backward to something valued before it was money — is, read at its strongest, a launch theory too: its terminus works once, at the origin, and retires. Both grand traditions are cold-start accounts promoted into value theories, one pointing backward, one forward. Demote both, keep both engines.
- *The math is brutal, now with the proof.* In prose: in any economy with a last day, token money unravels from the end — worthless then, so refused the day before, all the way back to now. A tax payable in the token blocks the unraveling — but only if the take is large enough against the stock; a sink smaller than the stock supports *nothing*, not a lower value. The engine has a threshold. Colonial Taiwan, around 1895, ran the failure case: taxes demanded in Japanese money to force the currency in; people deferred their taxes until the policy collapsed. The state was too small at the point of acceptance. Decree is not size.
- *Born pre-sold.* Most modern money never faces the cold start at all: a bank deposit is created in the same act as the dated obligation that will extinguish it, so its first and last acceptor is signed up at birth. Only orphan money — spent in with no return path — needs converts. One line to carry: *bank money is born pre-sold; only orphan money needs believers.*
**Payoff:** "the math is brutal" earns its title; Bitcoin's rehabilitation in this chapter sharpens (it cold-started with no terminus at all, once, and pays for it in volatility forever); and the Hayek section gains its missing diagnosis — his ducats lacked an engine *and* a focal point.

## P2 — strong additions, already story-shaped

### Ch 4 · the sentence that separates the rope from the tax
**Where:** the ledger/memory section of the draft, where the BoE loans-create-deposits material already lives.
**Why:** the deepest one-line taxonomy in the whole literature was cut from the published version of the money-is-memory paper and survives only in the working paper: money provides memory; bonds provide memory *and commitment*. The book's rope is memory with no bond anywhere; a tax is a bond wired into the police power. The two central objects of the book sit on the two sides of that cut sentence.
**Beat:** state it as the boundary: a future obligation is not more money — it is a ledger entry plus the threat of enforcement. Then the reframe the session earned: most modern money is *both at once* — a deposit (memory) matched at birth to a loan (commitment) — which is why it needs no believers (Ch 6's beat) and why its supply breathes (Ch 18's).
**Payoff:** Ch 13's clearing discipline and Ch 6's born-pre-sold both hang off one sentence installed here.

### Ch 13 · the hut tax, verified — a swap, not an addition
**Where:** the existing hut-tax case.
**Why:** the session verified the record against the colonial-Africa literature and came back with the administrator's own words: Governor Sir Percy Girouard — "We consider that taxation is the only possible method of compelling the native to leave his reserve for the purpose of seeking work." It also found the design detail that clinches the chapter's reading: some taxes were dischargeable by *proof of having worked* for European employers — a "labor tax," in the period's own vocabulary — so the purpose was visibly not revenue.
**Beat:** swap any paraphrased intent for the Girouard sentence; add the labor-tax discharge detail if it fits the word budget.
**Care:** Part IV symmetry is load-bearing — net +0 to +40 words; trim the surrounding paraphrase to pay for the quote. Mechanism, not indictment, as the first weave plan already ruled.

### Ch 19 · two ways a money dies, and the engineering that revives one
**Where:** the chapter's existing collapse arc.
**Why:** the session's registers give the chapter a taxonomy it currently implies but never states: a money can fail at the *level* (inflation — the subject of Ch 18) or at *selection* (flight, dollarization — this chapter), and the tools for one do not work on the other. Interest rates act on the level; they are nearly useless once acceptance itself is unravelling.
**Beat:** name the two failures; then the repair as terminus engineering, inheriting Ch 18's Sargent/Rentenmark beat — you do not talk a network back into a money, you re-attach an edge: a door that must open, a bound issuer. The Swiss dinar arrives from Ch 5 as the standing counterweight — a network that lost its terminus and did *not* die, holding on expectation alone for a decade — which is why selection failure is a failure of *belief about others*, not of machinery.
**Ladder rule:** this escalates to state scale; name the rung — the personal stack (Ch 21) is what one person does while the door is being rebuilt.

### Ch 22a · the debts paragraph
**Where:** after "The ones who told us what money is" (Innes, Kocherlakota, Mehrling).
**Why:** the session stood on shoulders the intermezzo doesn't name, and the naming *is* the book's evidence rule.
**Beat, compressed to the intermezzo's register:** the ones who found the door. Fullarton and Tooke, who saw in 1844 that notes issued on loan must return when the loan falls due, lost the vote, and were vindicated by the issuing institution a hundred and seventy years later. Lerner, who wrote the whole relay in one 1947 sentence — everyone accepts the paper because they know the taxpayers will. Starr, who proved in 1974 exactly what the tax can and cannot do: block the unraveling, never set the worth, and only if the take covers the stock. And the twist that belongs to this book's favourite economist: the man who proved money is memory co-wrote, one year later, the paper showing fiscal policy is an *equilibrium rejection device* — selection, not value — with the word "cause" held in quotation marks. Sargent, who showed the great inflations ended abruptly, on belief. Glasner, who first wrote down that the old regression looks backward while the tax looks forward — one step short of noticing they are the same move.
**Payoff:** the intermezzo's thesis — "that this is assembled from people who spent their lives on it is exactly why you should believe it" — extends to the newest layer.

### Epilogue · the fraction, where mathematics is allowed
**Where:** the machinery chapter, near the executable-canon material.
**Why:** the body's "floorless but fruitful" now has a one-line formalization, and the Epilogue is the room the book built for exactly this.
**Beat:** V = ℓ / (r + δ) — the value of money is the flow of marginal trade surplus it unlocks, discounted by patience and by the hazard that the acceptance network dies. Each holder harvests one period's ℓ and sells the remaining stream. Every comparative static in the book falls out: thicker network raises ℓ for everyone at once (the pull); more tokens meet ever-more-marginal trades (quantity discipline, derived); the obligation family enters only through δ (the guarantee, never the dividend); and the margin is observable — the foregone interest rate is the market's daily quote for ℓ. Close with the contrast that settles the century's quarrel in one clause: the fiscal theory discounts the state's surpluses; this book discounts the traders'. The dispute over "backing" was always a dispute over whose stream is in the numerator.

After application, three adversarial lenses were run on the emitted build (pacing, tic audit, voice). All three returned *better with repairs*, and the repairs were applied and re-verified against a rebuilt `Content.md` (~59,863 words — the repairs removed ~160 words of throat-clearing):

- **The tic audit found seventeen instances, most pre-dating this weave.** The full "and it is a good one" / "exact about" / "before we go on" / honesty-label family was removed across the Preface, Chs 2, 5, 6, 19, 21, 22, 23, the epilogue and the Field Book. (Ch 9's "Notice, too, that the London remittance…" was inspected and kept — it is an observation-marker leading into substance, not a concession frame.)
- **Voice.** The seventh question was cut to card rhythm and its two imperatives removed — the chapter's one-instruction promise ("watch the writer, not the spender") holds; the itemized-bill standard lives only on the Field Book card, where ways-of-doing are licensed. The intermezzo's new paragraph lost its two journal phrases ("rejection device", "against the stock") and its premature sole-contribution claim, which had contradicted the intermezzo's own ending sixteen lines early.

**Forks and findings during execution.** (1) Ch 18 contained a factual casualty the plan had not predicted: the draft treated Weimar as trust "gone for a generation," when Weimar is the fastest repair on record — the Sargent material *corrected* the chapter rather than merely enriching it, and the Zimbabwe half of the old sentence was kept. (3) The Mises sweep found the book already at the recension's "prior price" standard everywhere it speaks of him — the epilogue's A.10 meets him at his strongest via the diamond concession, and the appendix's "commodity terminus" correctly names only what is declined. No edits from the sweep. (4) Ch 23 turned out to be a questions chapter, not a glossary — the vocabulary became a *seventh question* ("Who owns the door, and what do they charge?"), mirrored onto the Field Book card, rather than glossary entries.
