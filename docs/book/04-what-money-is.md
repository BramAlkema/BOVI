# Chapter 4: What Money Is

> *Money is a portable forgetful ledger satisfying five minimal axioms. Most "properties of money" textbooks list are special cases or category errors.*

**Part II — Money as Coordination Infrastructure** · [Index](README.md) · Prev: [Chapter 3](03-consent-as-orthogonal.md) · Next: [Chapter 5](05-why-it-has-value.md)

## Opening scaffold

A young European tourist (unnamed) tries to pay €5 for coffee in Buenos Aires and gets charged €23. The mechanism is unclear; the friction is total.

## Structural beats

1. The €23 coffee.
2. How it happened — multiple exchange rates, card processor games, hidden spreads.
3. The realisation: the number on the card is not the same thing as money.
4. What is money, then? — the portable-forgetful-ledger definition.
5. The five axioms (transferable, sufficiently divisible, sufficiently verifiable, common knowledge that enough counterparties will keep accepting it).
6. The textbook list compared and reduced — "scarcity" is a special case of known supply; "stable in value" is the unit-of-account function bleeding in.
7. The cleaned-up list.

## Themes

- Definitions decide everything downstream.
- Folk taxonomies of money are confused.
- Honest first principles produce shorter lists.

## Twists

- The reader expects a textbook chapter on what money is. Instead the chapter explains that the textbook list is wrong.
- "Scarcity" — long treated as a defining property of good money — gets demoted to a footnote.

## Subarcs

- The portable-forgetful-ledger framing seeds the digital-surveillance discussion in Chapter 16 (the forgetfulness is increasingly fictional).
- The five axioms get reapplied to crypto, mobile money, and CBDC across Part II and Part VI.
- First appearance of the 4.7× credit card story (returns Chs 11, 16). Here it serves as friction-as-diagnostic.

## Closing scaffold

We know what money IS. We do not yet know why it has VALUE. The next chapter handles that.
