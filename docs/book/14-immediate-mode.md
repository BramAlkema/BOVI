## Draft

It is three hours into Sunday lunch, and Doña Elena will not let anyone pay.

This is the same woman we watched at dawn, moving money across five instruments with the cool precision of a treasury desk — pesos timed to the last legal day, dollars into stablecoin, a loan to a friend's business, a private exchange rate held in her head. All morning she was the most sophisticated financial operator in this book. And now, at the table, surrounded by the people she loves, she has switched into a mode where counting is not merely unnecessary but *forbidden.* To let a guest pay would not be thrifty. It would be an insult. The lunch, unlike everything else in her day, will not be tracked at all.

### The mode of not counting

Immediate mode is sharing without a ledger. Its tell is the gentlest of the four — not *is the price right,* not *are we even,* not *who decided,* but simply *are we okay?* It is the mode of family, of close friendship, of any real community: the pooled life of people who give to each other without keeping score, because keeping score would damage the very thing the giving is for. You do not invoice your child for dinner. You do not settle up with your closest friend. You do not, at a good lunch, let a guest pay. The whole point of these relationships is that they are *not* transactions, and to treat them as transactions is to end them.

It is, in a real sense, the most important mode — the one that holds together everything the other three cannot reach, where most human beings spend most of their lives. And here is something the reader who expected sophisticated people to be cold should notice: Doña Elena runs Immediate mode *cleanly.* Her fluency with money has not hollowed out her warmth. The opposite, if anything — knowing exactly which things are transactions seems to make her freer about the things that are not. Operating Value mode well and operating Immediate mode well are not in tension. They are the same skill, which is knowing which mode you are in.

### The most exploitable mode

But Immediate mode has a terrible vulnerability, and it is the same property that makes it precious: *it does not count.* The defence of every other mode is a kind of accounting — Value checks the price, Balanced checks the ledger, Obligated checks the authority. Immediate mode, by definition, refuses to check anything. And a relationship that will not keep accounts can be looted by anyone who knows it.

This is why *not counting* is also the weakness, and the abuses cluster around it. The first is the **fake family**: the workplace, the "community," the influencer, the institution that flies the Immediate banner — *we're a family here, we're all in this together* — in order to extract Immediate-mode generosity (unpaid overtime, emotional labour, loyalty, the extra mile) while operating, on payday, in unmistakable Value mode. It is the most lucrative confusion of modern work, because Immediate mode's own defence — *we don't count* — is exactly what stops the exploited person from counting what is being taken.

The second is the **silent ledger**: the friend always in need and never reciprocating, the relative who only calls when something is required, the partner who has quietly decided the relationship runs one way. Immediate mode assumes mutual non-counting; it breaks when one party stops giving and keeps taking, and because no one is supposed to be keeping score, the imbalance can run for years before anyone names it. The third is **structural**: the household management, the emotional labour, the remembering of birthdays and booking of appointments and absorbing of everyone's moods — real work, made invisible precisely because *not counting* is its medium, and therefore unpaid, unshared, unthanked.

### The defence

The defence here is the most delicate of the four, because the whole value of the mode is that you do not normally apply it. You cannot run Immediate mode while auditing it; the auditing kills it. But you can keep one question in reserve, for when something feels wrong: *can I refuse without changing the relationship?* If you can decline the favour, the overtime, the loan, the weekend, and the bond is unharmed — it is genuinely Immediate, and you are safe inside it. If refusing would cost you the job, the friendship, the standing, the place — then it is not Immediate at all. It is Obligated or Value wearing Immediate's warm clothes, and the warmth is the disguise. The question does not ask you to keep books on your loved ones. It only asks, in the rare bad moment, whether the no is free. If it is, you are home. If it is not, you have found the exploitation, and you can name it.

We have all four modes now — each with its logic, its tell, its native rot, and its small defence. So far we have met them mostly one at a time, in clean examples. The world is not so tidy. In the world the modes collide, and most of the trouble money causes between people turns out to be a collision nobody could name. That is the next part of the book.
