# Chapter 14: Immediate Mode

> *"We don't count, we just give." The most exploitable mode, because its defence is its weakness.*

**Part IV — The Four Modes Up Close** · [Index](README.md) · Prev: [Chapter 13](13-obligated-mode.md) · Next: [Chapter 15](15-mode-selection-daily-life.md)

## Opening scaffold

Doña Elena, three hours into Sunday lunch, refuses to let anyone pay. The same woman who has spent the morning moving USDT and pesos and rent in dollars and a friend's loan and tax timing — refuses to let lunch be tracked.

## Structural beats

1. The lunch scene.
2. Definition of Immediate mode.
3. Where it lives (families, close friendships, real communities).
4. The tell: *"are we okay?"*
5. Trap one — fake families (workplaces, "communities", influencer extractive culture).
6. Trap two — silent ledgers (the friend who always needs help, the relative who stops calling).
7. Trap three — structural extraction (emotional labour, household management).
8. Defence — *"can I refuse without changing the relationship?"*

## Themes

- The strongest mode is also the most vulnerable.
- Care work is invisible because *not counting* is its definition — but its invisibility is what makes it exploitable.
- Operating Value mode well *requires* operating Immediate mode well.

## Twists

- Doña Elena — the protagonist of the framework, the woman of the five-layer iPhone — runs Immediate mode cleanly.
- Sophistication does not crowd out warmth. The reader who expected "smart financial people are cold" gets the opposite.

## Subarcs

- Doña Elena's third revisitation. Now examined in the mode the previous appearances did not foreground.
- The "mode shape vs consent" matrix from Chapter 3 returns implicitly: fake families are Immediate-shaped exchanges with Value-mode goals — *coerced* Immediate.
- Defensive vocabulary: the refuse-without-cost test.

## Closing scaffold

We have all four modes. In the world, they collide.
