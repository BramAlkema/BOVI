# Weave plan — the governance material into the book

Everything below came out of one working session on the executable canon. None of it gets a new chapter. The book is already nine subarcs deep; this material either lands inside an existing arc or becomes **subarc 10**, and anything that can't do one of those two things stays in the Machinery.

**Standing constraints.** Body prose is self-contained: no file paths, no `.sol`, no repo status columns. Contracts are named in italics or not at all. No mathematics in the body — the Epilogue carries it. Most chapter files are scaffolding, so most of this work is edits to *beats/themes/subarcs* sections; only the Epilogue, Appendix and Field Book need finished prose.

**The ladder rule (added after the Ch 17 material landed).** Part VII ascends — Ch 21 is what one person can do, Ch 22 what a group can do, Ch 23 the one instrument that reaches the scales neither artifact does. So: **any material that escalates the book's scale must name the rung that answers it, at the point where the problem is posed.** Unnamed, the escalation reads as the book failing to answer rather than as answering higher up, and the lowest rung gets pushed into apologising for the whole climb. The artifacts are meant to be small and to stop where they stop; the questions are what carry.

## New subarc 10 — the governance debt (who holds the pen)

*Removing the floor creates a debt. Anti-essentialism without a discipline reads as permission — money is arbitrary, so the powerful may do as they like. That reading confirms the goldbug's fear in the goldbug's own words. The arc pays the debt: safety was never in the substance, it was in the conduct, and the substance merely gave everyone permission to look away.*

Installed in Ch 5 (the debt incurred and named), tested in Ch 6 (the goldbug's demand conceded, his label refused), lived in Chs 13 and 17 (who sets the clearing rule, and in whose favour), turned outward in Chs 18–20 (crises as conduct failures, not nominal events), armed in Chs 22–23 (asymmetric visibility — watch the pen, not the purse), and formalised in the Epilogue (where the machine hands back to people).

Add as item 10 to the README's cross-book subarcs, and extend the Part II blurb: floorless-but-fruitful **and** the discipline that has to replace the floor.

## P1 — the book is materially weaker without these

### Ch 5 · "No floor never meant no number"
**Where:** inside the existing `### What it's worth: floorless, but fruitful`.
**Why:** a first-time reader hears *floorless* as *arbitrary*, and the strongest sceptical push is the correct one — you can estimate the discounted benefit of holding an extra unit, and the estimate is stable because economies have inertia. Concede it before it's forced. The number is real, computable and stable; every term in it is conditional on someone else's continued acceptance; nothing in the sum stands independent of other people. Fixed point, not anchor.
**Beat:** the language analogy — you can price what knowing English is worth to you, and the precision is no evidence English means anything by itself.
**Payoff:** the stability *is* the pull observed, so the sceptic's own premise becomes the book's evidence. And the estimate is most reliable exactly when it matters least — which is the bridge to Ch 19.

### Ch 5 · the debt incurred, at the chapter's close
**Where:** new closing beat, feeding `### The thing it never needed`.
**Why:** this is the hinge of subarc 10 and the reason Part VI–VII exist.
**Line to carry:** *backing lets you audit a thing; pull forces you to watch people — the substance never removed the need to watch, it gave everyone permission to look away.*
**Beat:** the reader's question, asked in their voice rather than answered pre-emptively — *then what stops them?* Leave it open. Ch 6 concedes the demand; Part VII arms it.

### Chs 22–23 · asymmetric visibility (the required guardrail)
**Where:** Ch 22 as a Tally design principle; Ch 23 as a vocabulary entry; restated in the Field Book.
**Why:** "know who's doing what" slides into surveillance in one step. If the book doesn't state the asymmetry, a hostile reader supplies the other reading and the framework reads as social credit.
**The rule:** visibility is aimed at whoever holds the pen — issuer, dial-turner, attestor, whoever calls the thing into motion — never at whoever holds the balance. Watch the writer, not the spender.
**Why it belongs to the reader:** this is the operational meaning of *a tool for the taxed, not the taxer*, and it is the whole difference between this framework and a credit score.

## P2 — strong additions, each already story-shaped

### Part VI (Ch 18 or 19) · Sen, and famine without a shortage
Aggregate food supply adequate; people starved because their claims on it failed. This is the purest statement in existence of the book's micro-over-aggregate position — the total was fine, the distribution of claims killed people — and it belongs in the part about macro as infrastructure failure. Handle as a witness, not a case study; no arithmetic in the body.

### Ch 13 (Obligated Mode), with a thread to Ch 17 · the colonial hut tax
A tax obligation denominated in a currency obtainable only by selling your labour to a mine. Outcome: forced proletarianisation and a migrant-labour system whose settlement patterns outlived the tax. It is the clearest Obligated-mode money in the book's reach, it demonstrates the book's own thesis at the scale of a region's social formation — demand for the record comes from an obligation to clear, not from substance — and it makes Ch 17's "politics of mode defaults" concrete and historical rather than contemporary and arguable.
**Care:** it is standard scholarship, not a provocation. Write it as mechanism, not indictment; the mechanism is damning enough.

### Epilogue · new section — where the machine hands back
The session's most original artefact and the answer to the book's most dangerous question (*so your system is trustless too?*). Four kinds of judgement, each needing a different guard: **adjudicative** (a dispute needs a decider), **epistemic** (what is the state of the world — never a vote), **normative** (who bears what — the affected, voting, with real exit), **constitutive** (who counts as a member at all — prior to the rest, and unsolved). Plus the finding: a register of judgement points is also a register of **capture** points.
**And the keeper.** Nothing in the machinery can act on its own; every clearing depends on someone choosing to call it. So the clearing-discipline claim is half-executable — the machine enforces *whether* a clearing is valid, never *that it happens*. Whoever runs that is load-bearing and unnamed. State it; it is the strongest thing in the section precisely because it is a limitation.

## P2+ — the depoliticisation of unemployment (promotes Mundell out of P3)

**The case in one line.** We used to do unemployment with monetary politics, and then, deliberately and over thirty years, we stopped.

**Why it belongs, and why it is subarc 10's hardest case:** it runs *toward* the book's prescription, not against it. Central bank independence is conduct governance done properly — take the pen off the politician, bind it to a published rule, print the minutes, make the writer watchable. That is the framework's own recommendation, implemented at continental scale. The book cannot treat it as a villain without contradicting itself, and should not want to: politically-set rates produced the 1970s.

**What it cost, on the book's own terms.** Independence buys *impersonal* and *symmetrically known* by surrendering *elastic*. And elasticity is not only a betrayal vector — it is a shock absorber. Remove it and asymmetric shocks still get absorbed; they get absorbed in bodies rather than prices. Southern Spain is where the adjustment went when the exchange rate could not take it.

**The sentence to build the material around:** *the pen was taken from someone accountable and given to someone credible — different virtues, both real, and the swap was rarely described as a swap.*

### What the book already has, and what it doesn't
- **Has:** "known ≠ fixed" and elastic supply as a *virtue*, argued hard in Ch 4 against the goldbug and the Bitcoiner — and Ch 4 already promises the payoff: *"We will watch a fixed supply turn into a cage later, when we meet the deflation it breeds."* This material is that promise, generalised from a capped token to a fixed *adjustment channel*.
- **Doesn't have:** any three-axis anchor grading, and no mention anywhere of Mundell, the Phillips curve, the natural rate, central bank independence or the statutory employment mandates. All of it is new.
- **Install only what's needed.** Do **not** import a three-axis taxonomy into the body — the book has no room for another framework. One clause is enough: an anchor can be impersonal and knowable and *still* be rigid, and rigidity has a price someone pays.

### Ch 17 · primary home — *The Politics of Mode Defaults*
Currently every case in the chapter is a domain default (healthcare, housing, education) argued between parties. It has no monetary case and nothing at continental scale.

**Beats to add:**
1. The historical arc, compressed and non-technical: full employment as a statutory goal (the 1944 UK employment White Paper, the US Employment Act of 1946), devaluation as an ordinary tool of government, the Phillips curve as an apparent menu that elections were for — then Friedman and Phelps closing the menu in 1968, stagflation confirming it, Kydland and Prescott supplying the constitutional argument in 1977, and the build-out: independence, inflation targeting from 1990, and finally a currency union that for members abolishes the lever nationally.
2. **The mode-default reading:** this is the largest mode default in the modern world, and nobody voted on it. It belongs in this chapter because that is precisely what the chapter is about.
3. **The consent reading — Q5 run over a genuine success.** *Who decided that, and could I refuse?* Depoliticisation is measured by how far a decision is placed beyond the vote. Getting an uncomfortable answer when the card is run over an achievement is the strongest available evidence that the framework is not a partisan instrument — which is exactly this chapter's stated theme, currently asserted rather than tested.
4. **The precision that stops it being a polemic:** the institutions did not all do it the same way. The Fed kept employment in its statutory mandate; the ECB's price-stability mandate is primary and employment secondary. That difference is a large part of why the Spanish case looks as it does.

**Twist to add:** the chapter's existing twist is that the reader expects the book to pick a side and it refuses. Here the refusal costs something, because most readers *do* have a side on central bank independence — and the book still declines, on the same grounds.

### Ch 19 · Mundell as the mirror case
Spain is not a collapse and must not be filed as one. Use it as the **inversion** of everything Ch 19 already does: Argentina's anchor was too loose and *value* fled; Spain's was too rigid for the region and *people* fled. One variable — elasticity — failing in opposite directions, with emigration as the adjustment where devaluation used to be. That earns Mundell a place in the chapter rather than bolting him on, and it pays off Ch 4's cage line.

### Ch 4 · one clause only
Extend the existing forward-reference so the cage promise points here as well as at deflation. No new argument in Ch 4; it is already carrying enough.

### README
Extend subarc 10 to run through Ch 17 (the largest unvoted mode default) and Ch 19 (rigidity as the mirror of debasement). Add to Part V's blurb that the part's biggest mode-default case is monetary and continental.

### Discipline while writing it
- **Neutral on response, partisan on visibility.** Do not argue for reversal. Argue only that a *distributional* choice was moved out of the chamber and relabelled a technical one, and that the people who bear it should be able to see that this is what happened.
- No graphs, no Phillips curve drawn, no NAIRU. The history carries it; the arithmetic stays in the Machinery.
- Southern Spain is a witness, not a case study. Whoever appears there gets the Doña Elena treatment — a person first.
- Watch the risk: this material could tip the book into a euro critique, which is not its subject and would date it. It stays a *consent* case.

Steps 1–3 are the ones that change how the book is read; they are done. Step 5 is the one that most improves a weak chapter. Steps 4, 6 and 7 make it richer and can wait.

**Repair added after step 5 landed.** Building the depoliticisation case gave Part V–VI a four-chapter run at continental scale (Chs 17–20) and exposed a handoff the arc did not have: subarc 10 paid off Ch 17 → Ch 19 and then jumped to Chs 22–23, skipping **Ch 21** — the one chapter where the reader's own standing is established. Closed with a beat and a closing prose section, *What the notebook does not reach*: the stack gives standing, not leverage; seeing points upward at the pen, arming points inward at your own arrangements; no programme offered. Subarc-10 entries also added to Chs 18 and 20, which the README had been claiming carried the arc when they did not.

**Step 6 — done, but it took two passes.** The first pass put the hut tax into Ch 13 as beats and subarcs only — *above* the `## Draft` line, which `assemble.py` strips — so it was recorded as built while being invisible to readers. The adversarial pass caught it; the prose now exists (*The same mechanism, at the scale of a country*, between the cueva and the defence), together with the distributional caution the cueva passage needed. **The lesson is now a standing rule: beats change what a writer does, only draft prose changes what a reader gets.** Sen went into **Ch 18**, not 19: the chapter is already about why an aggregate cannot tell you what happened to you, and Sen is that at maximum stakes, so it lands as the deep reason rather than as a second example. Beat 4b, a theme line, a subarc entry naming the micro-over-aggregate spine, and a prose section — *The famine with food in it*. Entered through the nine-year-old, never the dataset; the democracy-and-free-press claim carried at its narrow, information-shaped strength with the contest acknowledged. The subarc entry states the load explicitly: without this beat Part VI reads as the book changing altitude, with it the altitude never changed. The **hut tax** went into Ch 13 as beat 4b with two subarc threads — the Ch 6 bootstrap engine seen from underneath, and Ch 17 made concrete before it arrives.

**Step 4 — done.** Built as the Epilogue's **Part C — Where the machine hands back**, between the construction section and the Coda. Opens on the dangerous question and refuses it outright: nothing here is trustless, and the claim is the smaller one — judgement is not removed but *enumerated*, and a mechanism whose judgement points are listed is a different object from one whose judgement points have dispersed into the phrase *the system*. Then the four classes with their guards and their category errors (the epistemic-by-ballot error given the inflation-reading case; the normative-by-technocrat error pointed back at Ch 17), the self-audit finding that our own machinery routed almost everything to a vote, and three closers: a register of judgement points is a register of **capture** points read with a different intention; nothing in the machinery can **act**, so the clearing-discipline spine is only half executable and the caller is unnamed and load-bearing; and the **record's resolution is an authoring decision**, which for this framework is the master judgement point. Coda extended to name the third question; Part VIII blurb updated. No contract names, no paths — the section is self-contained.

**Step 7 — done. The plan is complete.** Two closing sections in the Appendix, before its Coda. *Demonstrations of reach*: led with the **admission rule** and the **rejects**, because the exclusions are what make the tier credible — six surviving entries, of which three (Sen, the hut tax, Mundell) the reader has already met as chapters, so the tier reads as the body's formal statement rather than a grab-bag. Clark carries it, on the ground that he defeats the commons consolation — a *secure sole owner* exterminates on arithmetic. Entries flagged in the canon as citation-unverified were kept out of book prose. *The ratio is the argument*: the minimality claim measured rather than asserted — two lines against roughly twenty guards against some fourteen hundred of governance and repair — closing on why *what is money, really?* always disappointed the people who asked it: they pointed at the two lines and expected the fourteen hundred to be inside.

**Standing note for the next pass.** The plan's own discipline held throughout: nothing got a new chapter, everything landed in an existing arc or in the Machinery. Anything future material cannot do one of those two things with, it does not get to do.
