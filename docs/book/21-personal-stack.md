# Chapter 21: The Personal Stack

> *You can build your own unbundled architecture using tools that already exist.*

**Part VII — Living the Unbundle** · [Index](README.md) · Prev: [Chapter 20](20-global-majoritys-unbundled-stack.md) · Next: [Chapter 22](22-the-tally.md)

## Opening scaffold

A young European tourist pulls a notebook out of her backpack at a café in Buenos Aires and writes down: *Big Mac €3.20, flat white €2, transit pass €8, haircut €4, share of room €120/wk*. Her first personal index. No app required.

## Structural beats

1. The notebook scene.
2. The personal PDA — what it is, what it does, why a notebook is enough.
3. Personal indices from things you actually buy.
4. The multi-pot wealth allocation (transaction pot, store pot, contract pot, gift pot).
5. Just-in-time conversion at point of purchase.
6. What this beats — anchoring, social proof, recency bias, every cognitive trick a price tag can use against you.

## Themes

- Defensive vocabulary becomes defensive practice.
- The minimum-viable unbundled stack is much smaller than the fintech industry would have you believe.
- You do not need permission, capital, or expertise to start — only attention.

## Twists

- The chapter expects the reader to assume "this requires apps and accounts and complexity."
- Instead, the answer is a notebook. The technology is optional. The discipline is the thing.
