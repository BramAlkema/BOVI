# Chapter 2: The Four Modes

> *Humans coordinate exchange through four distinct fairness modes, present in every domain — money is just where this is most studied.*

**Part I — The Anthropology of Exchange** · [Index](README.md) · Prev: [Chapter 1](01-two-coffees.md) · Next: [Chapter 3](03-consent-as-orthogonal.md)

## Opening scaffold

A bridal party fighting over who pays for what — the same fight everyone has had, presented without the framework so the reader can feel the confusion before we name it.

## Structural beats

1. Six bridesmaids shouting past each other.
2. We name what we just watched: four different modes asserted simultaneously.
3. Fiske's Relational Models in plain language.
4. The table — Value, Balanced, Obligated, Immediate.
5. Parking shows all four.
6. Sex shows all four (with appropriate care).
7. Healthcare, education, work all show all four.
8. The framework is not about money — money is just where it has been most studied.

## Themes

- People are not irrational; they are mode-shifting.
- What looks like chaos in everyday exchange is mode collision.
- Vocabulary is the precondition of clarity.

## Twists

- The reader thinks the chapter will be about money. It is not — it is about parking, sex, education, work. Money becomes one example among many.
- The deeper twist: the bachelorette fight had no villains. Everyone was reasoning correctly within their own mode.

## Subarcs

- Anthropology lineage threaded gently — Mauss, Polanyi, Sahlins, Fiske footnoted but not foregrounded.
- The bridal party returns in Chapters 12, 15, and 22 for different analytical work each time. First of four uses.
- First introduction of the four modes that organise the entire rest of the book.

## Closing scaffold

We have the modes. But mode alone does not tell us legitimacy. The next chapter introduces the second axis.
