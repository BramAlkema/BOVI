# Chapter 9: Transaction Costs Are the Real Economy

> *30–50% of GDP is coordination cost. Money infrastructure is the master cost-reducer.*

**Part III — Trade, Coordination, Production** · [Index](README.md) · Prev: [Intermezzo 8A](08a-ye-tally-rope.md) · Next: [Chapter 10](10-markets-as-one-of-four.md)

## Opening scaffold

A young migrant wants to send €100 home for a sibling's birthday. Western Union: 12% + bad rate ≈ €15 in fees. Bank: five days. USDT: 30 cents and 3 minutes. A working-week's wage in fees vs. half a coffee.

## Structural beats

1. The remittance scene.
2. Coase 1937 — why firms exist (to internalise transaction costs).
3. Williamson's typology.
4. Wallis & North on measuring the transaction sector.
5. M-Pesa adding ~2% to Kenyan GDP just by lowering friction.
6. What fintech actually is — ongoing transaction-cost reduction.
7. Why "high finance" and "remittance corridors" are the same problem at different scales.

## Themes

- The real economy is invisible.
- Firms are not production units — they are transaction-cost containment vessels.
- The rich-country invisibility of transaction cost is itself a luxury.

## Twists

- The reader has been taught that economics is about supply, demand, and price formation. The chapter argues those are *consequences* of transaction-cost structure.
- The actual subject is coordination cost. Markets are infrastructure for cost reduction, not abstract optimisers.

## Subarcs

- Mobile money returns as the modern testbed for the cost-reduction story.
- Sets up Chapter 10 (markets are one of four mechanisms — and the "best" one only when transaction costs are low enough).
- The remittance scene seeds Chapter 20's broader survey of the global majority's stack.

## Closing scaffold

Markets work brilliantly when transaction costs are low. When they are not, three other mechanisms quietly do the work.
