# Chapter 7: The Four Functions Are Four Problems

> *The textbook list of "functions of money" is not a description of money — it is a description of forced bundling. Each function wants different properties.*

**Part II — Money as Coordination Infrastructure** · [Index](README.md) · Prev: [Chapter 6](06-cold-start-problem.md) · Next: [Chapter 8](08-specialisation-gains.md)

## Opening scaffold

Doña Elena's Sunday afternoon, examined function-by-function instead of as a single operation. Every move she makes corresponds to one of the textbook's four functions, but she does each in a different instrument.

## Structural beats

1. Decomposition of Doña Elena's stack by function.
2. The textbook's four functions named and listed (medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, standard of deferred payment).
3. Each function's actual requirements (table of conflicting wants).
4. Why bundling them creates impossible trade-offs. The sharpest conflict is **medium vs store**: a thing good to *save* gets held onto, which makes it bad to *spend* — so medium-quality and store-quality pull against each other and cannot be co-optimised in one instrument. This is the deepest reason the bundle fails; the central bank's impossible-trinity problems are downstream of it.
5. The unbundled stack as the natural answer.
6. Why "is X really money" debates are category errors.

## Themes

- Bundling causes the confusion bundled definitions are supposed to resolve.
- Sophisticated users have always unbundled; only textbooks have not.
- The question "what is money" is itself bundled.

## Twists

- Doña Elena, who in Chapter 1 looked like she was performing virtuoso financial sophistication, turns out to be doing exactly what the textbooks said money should do — just with the four functions properly assigned to four different tools. The "complexity" was just unbundled-correctness.
- The textbook isn't wrong about which functions exist; it is wrong about insisting they belong in one instrument.

## Subarcs

- Doña Elena's second revisitation. Same scene, different analytical lens. Reader starts to understand the chapter structure: same protagonist case examined under different lenses across the book.
- The "is X really money" dissolution sets up cleaner treatment of crypto, mobile money, gift cards in later chapters.

## Closing scaffold

Now that we have the money frame, we can apply it to the rest of economics.
