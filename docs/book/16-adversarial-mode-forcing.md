# Chapter 16: Adversarial Mode Forcing

> *Modern systems often force transactions into Value mode whether the user agreed or not.*

**Part V — When Modes Collide** · [Index](README.md) · Prev: [Chapter 15](15-mode-selection-daily-life.md) · Next: [Chapter 17](17-politics-of-mode-defaults.md)

## Opening scaffold

The 4.7× credit card story again — but this time examined as algorithmic *imposition*. The tourist did not agree to a Value-mode pricing event. It was forced on her by a system that knew things about her.

## Structural beats

1. The 4.7× story, third use.
2. The buyer-obfuscation arms race — PDA-as-attacker vs PDA-as-defender, the inevitable adversarial dynamic.
3. Surveillance pricing, behavioural targeting, dynamic pricing (Uber surge, dating-app pricing by age).
4. *"We're a family here"* as Value-mode-disguised-as-Immediate (workplace extraction).
5. *"It's just market rate"* as a move to shut down a different conversation.
6. Defensive moves — name the basis, refuse the frame, invoke a different mode.

## Themes

- Technology is not neutral about modes.
- Algorithms have political effects even when the engineers do not intend them.
- Defensive vocabulary is also defensive *technology*.

## Twists

- The chapter shows that "personalised pricing" — which sounds vaguely positive — is actually Value-mode aimed at *you*, weaponised.
- And that "we're a family here" — which sounds warm — is actually Immediate-mode-as-camouflage for Value-mode extraction.

## Subarcs

- 4.7× story's third and final use.
- The Doña-Elena/Berlin-barista contrast returns implicitly — the rich-country reader is *more* exposed to algorithmic mode-forcing than the dollarised-country reader, because her environment is more digitised.
- The voluntary/coerced axis from Chapter 3 returns: forced Value mode is *coerced* Value, with the same legitimacy problem as coerced Obligated.

## Closing scaffold

At individual scale, mode-forcing is friction. At societal scale, it is politics.
