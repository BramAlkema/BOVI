# Chapter 5: Why It Has Value

> *Money has value because of common knowledge that enough counterparties will keep accepting it at roughly known terms. Three inputs feed this single property.*

**Part II — Money as Coordination Infrastructure** · [Index](README.md) · Prev: [Chapter 4](04-what-money-is.md) · Next: [Chapter 6](06-cold-start-problem.md)

## Opening scaffold

Mateo on a rooftop in Buenos Aires, unprompted, explaining why Bitcoin works and why the dollar works for the same reason.

## Structural beats

1. The rooftop monologue.
2. We extract the single axiom — common knowledge of acceptability.
3. Three inputs that feed it: anchored supply, network depth, issuer credibility.
4. Three anchor types — legal, natural, mathematical — equally valid.
5. Why the dollar works abroad with no US tax (network is global, anchor credible).
6. Why bad currencies fail (anchor breaks first, network frays second).

## Themes

- Value is not in the token; it is in the common knowledge surrounding it.
- The anchor type does not matter, only that an anchor exists.
- Chartalism and commodity theory are both partial answers to the same question.

## Twists

- The chapter implicitly corrects mainstream economics (which centres state coercion) AND crypto evangelism (which dismisses state). Both are right about something and wrong about claiming exclusivity.
- The dollar working abroad without US tax obligation is the killer counterexample to naïve chartalism.

## Subarcs

- First serious treatment of crypto — sympathetic but not evangelical.
- Sets up the cold-start problem in the next chapter (if value just requires acceptance, why does not every well-designed token bootstrap?).
- Mateo first appears here — recurring witness, not protagonist.

## Closing scaffold

Common knowledge is the answer. But common knowledge has to start somewhere. Chapter 6 is about why starting it is the hardest engineering problem in money.
