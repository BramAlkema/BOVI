# Weimar Hyperinflation (1921–1923)

## Background

German currency collapse following WWI reparations and fiscal crisis. The mark lost value at rates that have become the textbook hyperinflation example. By late 1923, prices doubled every few days; people carried wheelbarrows of cash to buy bread.

## BOVI dynamics

### Obligated mode (O) dominance
- War reparations imposed external obligation hierarchy
- Government printing to meet fixed nominal obligations
- Citizens caught between domestic and international authority claims
- Tax collection became impossible as money lost value
- The state had Obligated-mode authority but no real Value-mode tool

### Value mode (V) collapse
- Marks ceased functioning as transferable medium
- Price discovery broke down completely (prices changed by the hour)
- Barter networks emerged as V-mode alternative
- Foreign currencies (dollars, gold) became real V-mode money
- Common knowledge of supply was destroyed by uncontrolled printing

### Balanced mode (B) chaos
- Wages/prices repriced daily, breaking accounting systems
- No stable unit for tracking obligations across time
- Contracts became impossible to maintain
- Pension and savings ledger systems collapsed into meaninglessness
- Inter-generational wealth transfers obliterated

### Immediate mode (I) survival
- Communities reverted to direct reciprocity
- Food more valuable than financial assets
- Personal relationships replaced institutional trust
- Local mutual aid networks provided stability where institutions had failed

## Bundle lesson

When O-mode obligations conflict with V-mode scarcity, **the system collapses into I-mode survival networks**. Recovery (Rentenmark, 1923) required new O+V bundle with credible scarcity constraint — explicitly anchored to land value to restore common knowledge.

This is the canonical case of **inflation as broken common knowledge**. Once supply credibility is destroyed, no policy lever inside the same currency can restore it. A new currency with a credible new anchor was needed.

## Pattern repetition

The Weimar pattern has repeated in:
- Hungary (1946 — fastest hyperinflation in recorded history)
- Yugoslavia (1992-1994)
- Zimbabwe (2007-2008)
- Venezuela (ongoing)
- Lebanon (2019–present)
- Argentina (multiple times, ongoing — see [Argentina case](argentina-bigmac-dollarisation.md))

In each, the same sequence:
1. **Anchor wobble** — official numbers diverge from lived experience
2. **Network fray** — sellers stop accepting local currency for big items
3. **Function migration** — store-of-value escapes first, unit-of-account follows, medium-of-exchange last
4. **Local currency reduces to its Obligated floor** — held only for tax

Survivors run unbundled stacks (foreign currency, real assets, gift networks) by necessity. See [Argentina case](argentina-bigmac-dollarisation.md) and [global majority's stack](mobile-money.md).

## What this case teaches the framework

1. **Common knowledge of supply is the load-bearing axiom** — destroy it and the currency cannot be repaired from inside
2. **The four modes do not disappear when one collapses** — they migrate to other instruments
3. **I-mode emerges as the survival floor** — when institutions fail, communities improvise
4. **Recovery requires a new bundle, not a fix to the old one** — Rentenmark needed land anchor; old mark could not be saved
5. **The unbundling pattern is forced by failure** — survivors of hyperinflation are involuntary BOVI practitioners
