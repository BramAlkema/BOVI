# Fiske's Relational Models Theory

## Definition

Alan Page Fiske's **Relational Models Theory (RMT)**, developed in *Structures of Social Life* (1991), identifies four elementary forms of sociality that appear universally across human cultures.

Fiske's own doctrine is **innately prepared, culturally completed**: each abstract structure (a "mod") requires learned cultural parameters ("preos") specifying how, when, and with whom it applies — "the models themselves are not sufficient to determine behavior or cognition" (his words). "Not learned behaviours" would overstate him. The innateness evidence is also uneven across the four: strongest for Authority Ranking and Communal Sharing (infant studies, phylogenetic precursors), open for Equality Matching, and **absent for Market Pricing** — which cuts the framework's way, since Posture #1 already refuses market logic as the universal. When someone violates the wrong mode for a relationship, we feel visceral disgust (your boss eating off your plate feels creepy in a way that's hard to articulate) — and that violation result is RMT's best-replicated empirical leg.

BOVI is the application of Fiske's RMT to monetary exchange.

## The four relational models

### 1. Communal Sharing (CS) — *"What's mine is yours"*
- **Principle**: resources flow according to need and availability within the group
- **Fairness logic**: "From each according to ability"
- **Emotional signature**: unity, belonging, shared identity
- **Violations feel**: selfish, exclusionary
- **BOVI mapping**: **Immediate** mode

### 2. Authority Ranking (AR) — *"Rank determines privilege"*
- **Principle**: resources flow according to hierarchical position
- **Fairness logic**: "Noblesse oblige"
- **Emotional signature**: respect, deference, legitimate authority
- **Violations feel**: insubordinate, presumptuous
- **BOVI mapping**: **Obligated** mode

### 3. Equality Matching (EM) — *"Tit-for-tat"*
- **Principle**: resources flow to maintain balance and reciprocity
- **Fairness logic**: "Equal treatment"
- **Emotional signature**: justice, symmetry, turn-taking
- **Violations feel**: unfair, unbalanced
- **BOVI mapping**: **Balanced** mode

### 4. Market Pricing (MP) — *"Value for value"*
- **Principle**: resources flow proportional to some ratio or rate
- **Fairness logic**: "Proportional contribution"
- **Emotional signature**: efficiency, rationality, optimisation
- **Violations feel**: irrational, wasteful
- **BOVI mapping**: **Value** mode

## The mapping at a glance

| Fiske model | Symbol | BOVI mode | Measurement scale (Fiske's own) | Clearing discipline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CS (Communal Sharing) | CS | **Immediate** | nominal | never clears |
| AR (Authority Ranking) | AR | **Obligated** | ordinal | clears on schedule, amount prescribed by rank or occasion |
| EM (Equality Matching) | EM | **Balanced** | interval | nets over a window; rough equivalence, not a price |
| MP (Market Pricing) | MP | **Value** | ratio | clears now, at a price, between strangers |

The scale column is Fiske's own correspondence to Stevens's levels of measurement (Fiske 1991; 1992) — carried at its actual strength: an analogy he drew ("correspond closely," in sources' words "resembles"), absent from his own summary essays, never formalised or empirically tested in the decades since, and whose *nesting* corollary failed the one direct test (Dien, Karuzis & Haarmann 2018, fMRI: activations did not follow the cumulative hierarchy — external corroboration of this repo's own scale-ladder kill). It remains useful as the modes-as-resolutions *frame* — the canonical statement, marked "frame not stone," is `FOUNDATIONS.md` §"modes as clearing disciplines".

## Market Pricing is proportionality — money is one instance of it

Fiske defines Market Pricing by the **ratio scale**, and says so directly:

> "Market Pricing (MP) relationships are oriented to socially meaningful ratios or rates such as prices, wages, interest, rents, tithes, or cost-benefit analyses. **Money need not be the medium**, and MP relationships need not be selfish, competitive, maximizing, or materialistic."

His MP examples include cost-effectiveness standards, utilitarian judgments, equity in proportion to contribution, and spending time efficiently — no money in any of them. Prescribed transfers, meanwhile, live *inside* the other models: "offerings of filial piety" under AR, "restitution in kind" under EM. Money-like transfers run across all four models; MP owns the *ratio relationship*, not the instrument.

**The consequence for BOVI.** Fiske partitions *kinds of relationship*; monetary functions partition *uses of one instrument*. Those are orthogonal cuts, so the modes map onto his scale types (the table above) and onto nothing instrument-shaped. What the framework may legitimately claim is a correspondence between Fiske's relationship typology and the instrument typology of Polanyi, Dalton and Kuroda — **claimed as the framework's own contribution**, not as something found in Fiske ([special-purpose money](special-purpose-money.md); the framework's candidate correspondence was staked with kill conditions and killed by its specimen pass — post-mortem at [the scale ladder](../../SCALE-LADDER.md)). An earlier version of this file carried a fourth table column assigning each model a monetary function; no published source makes that mapping, and it is retired — record in `MODE-FUNCTION-MAPPING-PLAN.md`.

## What RMT gives BOVI, at the evidence's strength

Audited against the literature (2026), the foundation holds at a *more precise* strength than the usual rhetoric:

1. **Four discrete types — supported.** Haslam's taxometric programme shows relationships are represented as discrete types, not continuous dimensions. Four *clean orthogonal factors* is harder: the original confirmatory factor analysis fit badly (RMSEA .243), CS and EM correlate at .80–.85, and the good fit arrived only in 2023 (Zakharin & Bates) after dropping 13 of 33 items and adding a general factor RMT does not predict — though merging CS and EM *worsened* fit, which is genuine discriminant evidence. Use "four types," not "four primitives."
2. **Proposed as universal; tested mostly in the West.** The base is Fiske's Moose (Burkina Faso) fieldwork plus a synthesis of the wider ethnographic record; the controlled cross-cultural test is five diaspora groups resident in the US; the psychometric validations are Western samples, and the validators themselves say non-Western replication is still required.
3. **Descriptive, not prescriptive** — holds, and is the framework's real dependency.
4. **Pre-economic — for three of the four.** Phylogenetic precursors exist for CS and AR, EM is open, and **MP has none**; MP is also absent from the developmental literature and culturally variable in its distribution. The framework may not argue "the Value mode is a pre-economic primitive" — and does not need to: the *least* wired-in mode being the market one is Posture #1's point made for it.
5. **Alive** — RMT remains generative (relationship-regulation theory 2011; *Theory and Society* extensions 2026); its best-replicated leg is the taboo-trade-off/visceral-violation result the canon already uses in Quirk 15.

## The visceral-violation argument

A key piece of Fiske's evidence: when modes are violated, people feel *visceral disgust*, not just intellectual disagreement.

- Your boss eating off your plate (CS violation in an AR context) → creepy
- A friend submitting an itemised bill for hosting you (MP violation in a CS context) → cold, alien
- A government bureau treating you as a friend (CS violation in an AR context) → corrupt
- A relative paying you for time spent listening (MP violation in a CS context) → insulting

These feelings are diagnostic. They tell us the modes are real cognitive structures, not invented categories.

## Why Fiske's framework matters more than other taxonomies

Many social-science frameworks have offered typologies of relationships (Parsons, Habermas, Luhmann, etc.). What distinguishes RMT:

1. **Breadth of grounding** — Fiske's own fieldwork is the Moose of Burkina Faso; the universality claim rests on that plus his synthesis of the wider ethnographic record (a previous version of this file said "fieldwork extended to 100+ cultures," which is false — the 100+ is literature synthesis, not fieldwork)
2. **Cognitive grounding** — the claim is about cognition, not culture, in the mod/preo form: innate structures completed by learned cultural parameters
3. **Generative power** — the four modes generate predictions about which institutional forms will arise where
4. **Affect grounding** — the modes have characteristic emotions, which makes them empirically falsifiable; the taboo-trade-off result is the best-replicated instance

## How BOVI extends Fiske

Fiske's RMT describes social relationships in general. BOVI applies it specifically to **monetary exchange**, which is just one (very important) domain of relationship structuring.

The BOVI extension claims:
- Money is a *bundle* of all four modes (not just MP / Value)
- Each mode's monetary function is genuinely distinct (medium of exchange, ledger, hierarchical extraction, gift)
- Most monetary debates are mode disputes in disguise
- Conscious bundling beats unconscious bundling
