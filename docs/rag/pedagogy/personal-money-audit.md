# The Personal Money Audit

A guided self-reflection exercise that builds the insight **"I can choose my bundles consciously"** ([grasp 5](insights-to-grasp.md)).

## The setup

Walk users through their current money behaviours across different life contexts. Help them identify which fairness mode dominates each, where they're already mode-switching skilfully, and where they're stuck in one mode inappropriately.

## The audit questions

For each life context, the user identifies the dominant mode they currently use:

1. *"How do you handle money with family?"* → which mode dominates?
2. *"How do you approach workplace finances?"* → different mode?
3. *"What's your investment philosophy?"* → different mode?
4. *"How do you think about taxes?"* → different mode?
5. *"What's your charitable giving approach?"* → different mode?

Most users discover they already use *all four modes*, just unconsciously and not always in the contexts where each works best.

## Pattern recognition

After the audit, look for patterns:

- **Where do you mode-switch successfully?** (e.g., Immediate with family, Value with strangers — usually the well-functioning areas of your money life)
- **Where are you stuck in the wrong mode?** (e.g., Balanced with romantic partner — keeping a secret ledger; Value with charity — calculating "ROI" on giving)
- **Which mode is under-developed?** (e.g., never use Immediate; treat everything as transactional; or never use Value; can't bargain effectively)

## Agency building

> *"Now that you can see your patterns, where would you like to make different choices? What modes feel under-developed in your money relationship?"*

This shifts the user from passive money behaviour to **conscious mode design**. Examples of audit-driven changes:

- **Recognising over-Balanced friendships** → choose to gift more, count less
- **Recognising over-Immediate workplace** → push back on "we're a family here" extraction
- **Recognising under-Value bargaining** → start asking for what things actually cost
- **Recognising mis-applied Obligated** → notice when you're complying with authority that hasn't earned it

## Tool recommendations

Based on the user's mode preferences and development areas, the audit can suggest specific financial tools, apps, or approaches that support their fairness style. For example:

- User over-balanced in friendships → introduce [the Tally](../practice/the-tally-product.md) (mode-aware ledger that includes Immediate as first-class)
- User stuck in mental-arithmetic price comparisons → introduce [personal PDA](../practice/personal-pda.md) (write it down, stop guessing)
- User holding everything in current account → introduce [unbundled personal stack](../practice/unbundled-personal-stack.md) (multi-pot architecture)

## What this experience is NOT

- Not a financial-planning session (the framework is mode-neutral on which instruments to use)
- Not a therapy session (though the audit may reveal therapeutic territory)
- Not a values assessment (each mode is equally valid; the question is whether you're *using* it appropriately for the context)

It is **diagnostic**, in the same sense that a doctor's audit is diagnostic — looking at what is, not prescribing what should be.

## Why this experience works pedagogically

1. **Personal data** — the audit is about the user's actual life, not hypothetical scenarios
2. **Pattern recognition** — humans are good at spotting patterns once data is structured
3. **Agency emergence** — recognition naturally produces the question *"what could I do differently?"*
4. **No expert required** — the user knows their own behaviour better than anyone else; they just need the framework to organise it
5. **Replays in life** — once installed, the audit lens runs continuously in the background

## The deeper lesson

The audit reveals that users have *always* been making mode choices in their money behaviour — they just didn't have the vocabulary to see them as choices. Calling them out as choices is what shifts them from default to conscious.

> *"Now that you can see your patterns, you can change them — or keep them, but knowingly."*

This is the framework's promise in distilled form. Not to make you do anything different, but to make your choices visible to yourself.

## What the BOVI app should do

Per `EDUCATIONAL-GAP-ANALYSIS.md`, the existing BOVI app does not have audit experiences. The Personal Money Audit is what should be on M2 (the third user complexity layer) — for users who have completed the [$20 Bill Revelation](twenty-dollar-revelation.md) and [Empathy Machine](empathy-machine.md), and are ready to apply the framework to their own life.
