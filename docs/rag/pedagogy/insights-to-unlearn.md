# Five Insights to Unlearn

The BOVI educational mission requires **fundamental mental-model transformation** — not learning new facts, but unlearning deeply embedded assumptions about money and fairness that feel like natural truths but are actually cultural constructs.

## ❌ Unlearn 1: "Money is a Single Thing"

- **Current model**: money is a unified economic tool with one purpose (store of value, medium of exchange, unit of account rolled into one coherent system)
- **What's wrong**: this obscures that money serves *multiple, often conflicting* fairness functions simultaneously
- **Pedagogical challenge**: people experience money as one thing because it's presented as one thing
- **Approach**: interactive scenarios where users see the *same transaction* interpreted through four different fairness lenses

## ❌ Unlearn 2: "My Money Preferences are Rational"

- **Current model**: "I prefer Bitcoin / cash / credit cards because they're objectively better for X reason"
- **What's wrong**: preferences often reflect *unconscious fairness mode biases*. Someone who hates debt might be strong in Immediate mode. Someone who loves credit optimisation might be strong in Value mode.
- **Pedagogical challenge**: people experience their money preferences as *rational economic choices*, not as expressions of moral psychology
- **Approach**: fairness mode assessment that helps users identify their unconscious biases

## ❌ Unlearn 3: "Economic Debates are About Economics"

- **Current model**: inflation hawks vs MMT advocates are arguing about *technical economic mechanisms*
- **What's wrong**: they're actually defending *different moral intuitions* about how resources should flow in society. The technical arguments are rationalisations for deeper fairness preferences.
- **Pedagogical challenge**: people interpret passionate disagreement as evidence that *someone must be factually wrong*, rather than that people have different fairness mode priorities
- **Approach**: case studies showing how the same economic data gets interpreted differently based on fairness mode emphasis

## ❌ Unlearn 4: "Fair = My Definition of Fair"

- **Current model**: fairness has a universal definition; people who disagree are either ignorant or malicious
- **What's wrong**: there are *four distinct, equally valid fairness intuitions* that can contradict each other. What feels fair in one mode can feel deeply wrong in another.
- **Pedagogical challenge**: people's fairness intuitions feel *viscerally correct*. Asking them to consider other modes feels like asking them to accept unfairness.
- **Approach**: start with contexts where they naturally use multiple modes (family vs workplace vs marketplace) and help them recognise they *already* mode-switch instinctively

## ❌ Unlearn 5: "Monetary Reform Should Optimise One Thing"

- **Current model**: the goal is to find the *one correct monetary system* that maximises efficiency / fairness / stability
- **What's wrong**: any functioning monetary system must serve *all four fairness modes simultaneously*. Optimising for one inevitably creates tensions in others.
- **Pedagogical challenge**: people want *simple solutions* to complex problems. Accepting money as an inherent bundle of trade-offs feels unsatisfying.
- **Approach**: historical analysis showing how *every* monetary system has bundled all four modes, just in different proportions

## The teaching sequence

These five unlearnings are paired with five [insights to grasp](insights-to-grasp.md). Together they constitute the framework's educational core. The book's job is to *enact* this transition without lecturing.

The framework's pedagogical commitment: **recognition before analysis**. People need to *feel* the difference between modes before they can think about it analytically.
