# Balanced Mode (B)

## Definition

**"Are we even?"** The mode of friendship, peer ledgers, and symmetric reciprocity over time. Mapped from Fiske's **Equality Matching**.

The expectation is not payment but *evening up over time*. You spotted me €5 last week, I'll get the kebab tonight. You got the last beer, I'll grab the next round. There's an invisible ledger and both sides know it's there.

## Where it lives

- Friendship and peer relationships (the dominant mode)
- Splitwise, splitting bills, taking turns driving
- Group-chat tabs, lending clothes, "I'll cook if you do dishes"
- Flatshare arrangements
- Carpools, mutual favours, "I owe you one"
- Most reciprocal exchanges where the relationship is valued

The point isn't exact equivalence — it's that nobody is *taking the piss*. Roughly even over time = relationship intact.

## Why it works

Friends don't want pure Value mode (cold, transactional) or pure Immediate mode (somebody always paying). The Balanced ledger lets you exchange without the relationship feeling weird.

It also scales to medium-trust groups: rotating savings clubs (susu in West Africa, chit funds in India), informal lending circles, hawala-style trust networks.

## The tell you're in Balanced mode

The question is **"are we even?"**

Not: "is the price right?" (Value)
Not: "do I have to?" (Obligated)
Not: "do we even count this?" (Immediate)

Symmetry over time.

## The trap: two failure modes

### 1. Score-keeping weaponised
"I did X for you, so you owe me Y." Now the ledger is being used to guilt-trip or extract, not to maintain a relationship. Once one side starts itemising, the mode is dead.

### 2. Unequal capacity faking equality
Splitting the bill evenly when one person earns 3× the other *looks* balanced but isn't. Same with housework, who organises everyone's birthday, who keeps the group chat warm. Equal split, unequal load.

## The creeping trap: tools that impose Balanced everywhere

Apps like Splitwise prevent the first failure (forgotten debts) but actively *create* a constant-ledger mindset that makes friendships feel transactional. The tool quietly converts Immediate exchanges into Balanced ones.

This is what motivates [the Tally product](../practice/the-tally-product.md) — a mode-aware alternative that lets users tag transactions Immediate (gift, no count) instead of forcibly converting everything to Balanced.

## The defence

Name the ledger out loud occasionally. If both sides can say what they're tracking and what counts as even, the mode works. If somebody's keeping a *secret* ledger — running internal scores you don't know about — that's the warning sign.

Periodically zoom out: even ledgers can hide unequal capacity.

## Voluntary vs coerced Balanced mode

| Voluntary | Coerced |
|---|---|
| Spotting a friend €5 | "I helped you, you owe me Y" guilt-tripping |
| Splitting a meal evenly with peers | Forced "equal" split where one person can't afford it |
| Joining a rotating savings club | Family pressure to participate in ledger you don't want |

## Historical and everyday examples

- **Rotating savings clubs** — susu, chit funds, tandas: each member takes the pot in turn, and the obligation is to keep taking your turn. Turn-taking is Equality Matching in its purest institutional form.
- **Favour ledgers between peers** — spotting a friend €5, covering a round, watching someone's children on the understanding it comes back
- **Splitting a meal evenly among peers**
- **Labour-exchange customs** — barn-raising, harvest rings, work parties where households owe each other days rather than money

### Where the neighbouring artifacts sit

Balanced mode is diagnosed by the exchange, not the equipment — [the diagnosis rule](../core/four-modes-overview.md): does a standing account stay open between peers, and is the amount a rough equivalence rather than a price? By that test: **proto-cuneiform tablets and English exchequer tallies sit in Obligated mode** — both record amounts prescribed by an authority (temple and palace dues; tax and tribute receipts). **Double-entry bookkeeping and distributed ledgers are mode-agnostic** record technologies, carrying whichever discipline is written into them; one ledger can hold all four disciplines at once, which is exactly what [the Tally](../practice/the-tally-product.md) is built to do. **The settlement rope sits in Value** — it prices and clears between strangers ([ruling](../practice/the-tally-product.md#mode-classification)).
