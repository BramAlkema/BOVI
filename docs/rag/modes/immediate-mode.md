# Immediate Mode (I)

## Definition

**"We don't count, we just give."** Gift, care, sharing without expectation of return. Mapped from Fiske's **Communal Sharing**.

The transaction is invisible because the *relationship* is what matters; tracking would insult it.

## Where it lives

- Tight families (the canonical home)
- Close friendships (real ones)
- Small communities with strong bonds
- Mutual aid networks
- Anywhere the cost of breaking the bond is much higher than the cost of any individual exchange

Examples: family meals, helping a friend move, parents covering you when you're broke, the housemate who replaces the toilet roll without saying anything.

## The tell you're in Immediate mode

Nobody's keeping score and nobody *wants* to.

The question isn't "is this fair?" — it's **"are we okay?"**

If you catch yourself thinking *"I owe them for this"* in a way that surprises you, you might have left Immediate mode without noticing.

## The trap: the most exploitable mode

Immediate mode is the most exploitable mode because its defence — *not counting* — is also its weakness. It gets weaponised in three directions:

### 1. Fake families
- Bosses who say "we're a family here" right before asking for unpaid overtime
- Influencers using "community" to extract free promotion
- Gyms and schools framing themselves as "your tribe" so leaving feels like betrayal

They collect Immediate-mode goodwill while operating in Value mode underneath.

### 2. Silent ledgers
Someone *is* keeping score and using your gift-mode generosity to extract Value-mode wins:
- The friend who always "needs help" but never reciprocates
- The relative who stops calling once they got the loan

### 3. Structural extraction
Emotional labour, who remembers birthdays, who organises the household, who keeps everyone's relationships maintained. Often defaulted onto one person under the cover of *"we don't count this kind of thing."* Of course you don't count it — that's why it stays uncompensated.

This is often gendered (women doing emotional labour) or racialised (caregiving expected from particular communities) or class-coded.

## The defence

Ask whether you can **refuse without it changing the relationship**.

In real Immediate mode, you can give or not give, take or not take, and the bond holds. If saying no costs you the friendship, the job, the grade, the place to live — it's not Immediate, it's *Obligated wearing Immediate's clothes*. Naming that usually breaks the spell.

## The protagonist illustration

The book's recurring case is Doña Elena (58, Argentinian, the protagonist of the framework). The same woman who runs a sophisticated five-layer money stack on her iPhone refuses to let anyone pay for Sunday lunch. The framework's claim: operating Value mode well *requires* operating Immediate mode well. Sophistication does not crowd out warmth.

## Voluntary vs coerced Immediate mode

| Voluntary | Coerced |
|---|---|
| Giving a gift to your child | "We're family here" workplace extracting unpaid labour |
| Helping a friend move | Family pressure to provide free care work |
| Hosting a meal for your community | Workplace "community" expectations replacing pay |
| Choosing to share with neighbours | Implicit class/race/gender expectations of caregiving |

The mode shape is the same (no-count exchange in a relational context). The consent axis decides legitimacy. See [consent](../core/consent-axis.md).

## Historical examples (from theory.md)

- Village mutual aid systems
- Indigenous gift economy networks
- Modern local currency projects
- Neighbourhood favour exchanges
