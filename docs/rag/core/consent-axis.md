# Consent as the Orthogonal Axis

## Definition

**Mode classification describes the shape of an exchange. Consent provides legitimacy. They are separate axes.**

This is the conversation-derived refinement that disarms a critique: that BOVI flattens the ethical difference between, say, voluntary charity and coerced taxation by calling them both Obligated mode. The reply: yes, they have the same shape (compliance with authority); they differ on the *consent* axis, which is orthogonal.

## The eight-cell matrix

Each of the four modes can be voluntary or coerced. Eight cells, not four.

| Mode | Voluntary version | Coerced version |
|---|---|---|
| **Value** | shopping when you want to | being forced to sell your labour at any wage |
| **Balanced** | spotting a friend €5 | "I helped you, you owe me" guilt-tripping |
| **Obligated** | BDSM with safe words / monastic vows / volunteer military / apprenticeship | state tax / draft / arranged marriage / serfdom |
| **Immediate** | giving a gift freely | "we're family here" workplaces extracting unpaid labour |

## Voluntary Obligated mode (the surprising case)

The example that drives the orthogonal-axis insight: people *choose* Obligated relationships all the time, often *because* the authority structure provides what they want — discipline, safety, growth, pleasure.

Examples of consensually-entered Obligated mode:

- BDSM relationships (safe words, negotiated limits, aftercare)
- Religious orders taking vows of obedience
- Military enlistment (volunteer)
- Twelve-step programmes (submission to sponsor + higher power)
- Apprenticeships under a master
- Strict gym/coach/trainer relationships
- Martial arts dojos with rank hierarchies
- Joining a workplace with hierarchical management
- Competitive athletes with demanding regimens

In each case the *mode shape* is Obligated, but the *legitimacy* comes from the consent and exit conditions, not from the structure itself.

## What this fixes in the framework

Without the consent axis, the framework risks two errors:

1. **Conflating tax with charity** — both are someone moving money to a higher authority; same mode shape; ethically very different
2. **Conflating BDSM with conscription** — both are voluntary submission to authority; same shape; ethically very different

With the axis added: both pairs share *shape*, differ on *consent*. The framework gives you shape; consent gives you legitimacy. They're separable diagnostic axes.

## The political implication

Most political fights about institutions are not really about which mode applies. They are about whether the *consent and exit conditions* are met inside whichever mode dominates.

- "Should taxes be lower?" = a fight about Obligated-mode coercion's *severity*, given that everyone agrees taxes are Obligated mode
- "Should this workplace be unionised?" = a fight about whether Obligated-mode workplace authority has adequate consent / exit
- "Is this relationship abusive?" = a fight about whether Immediate-mode relational expectations are masking coerced extraction

Naming the consent axis lets these conversations proceed without first re-litigating the mode classification.

## Defensive vocabulary application

When encountering any mode-classified exchange, also ask:

- **Did everyone consent to entering?** (informed, capacity, alternatives)
- **Can they exit at acceptable cost?** (real exit options, not just nominal ones)
- **Is the consent fresh, or has the situation drifted from what was consented to?**

If consent + exit are present, the mode is legitimate regardless of which mode it is. If they're absent, the mode is illegitimate regardless of how prosocial the surface appears.
