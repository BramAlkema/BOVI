# The Unbundled Personal Stack

## Definition

The framework's practical operational claim: **anyone can build their own unbundled architecture using tools that already exist.**

You do not need permission, capital, or expertise. The minimum-viable unbundled stack fits in your phone and a notebook.

## The reference case: Doña Elena

Doña Elena (the framework's protagonist) is 58, Argentinian, no MBA, no financial training. She runs a five-layer money operation on her iPhone every Sunday afternoon:

| Function | Tool |
|---|---|
| Tax-currency floor | Pesos (held only as long as required) |
| Daily transactions | Pesos converted just-in-time, or USDT via Mercado Pago |
| Store of value | USDT, real estate, and a loan to a friend's furniture business |
| Long-term contracting | Rent denominated in dollars (even though she lives in Argentina) |
| Price discovery | Her own knowledge of the blue rate, the official rate, and what things actually cost |
| Care/gift exchange | Sunday lunch she insists on paying for, no tracking |

She is not exotic. She is *most of humanity*. The "complexity" of her operation is just unbundled-correctness — the four functions of money assigned to four different tools, plus a separate care layer.

## The minimum viable stack for a stable-currency reader

A 20-something European or American reader does not need Doña Elena's full stack because their local fiat works adequately for now. But the *minimum* unbundled stack is still useful as a defensive practice:

### Layer 1: Personal PDA (a notebook)
- Track 5–10 items you regularly buy
- Date, place, price, what you got
- → see [personal PDA](personal-pda.md)

### Layer 2: Personal index
- Use the PDA data as your own purchasing-power measure
- Trust your numbers over headline CPI
- → see [personal indices](personal-indices.md)

### Layer 3: Multi-pot wealth allocation
- **Transaction pot**: just enough to cover the next 1–2 weeks
- **Store pot**: appreciating assets (index funds, real estate, gold, BTC if you want, etc.) — *not* idle cash
- **Contract pot**: pre-funded for upcoming committed payments (rent, taxes, subscriptions)
- **Gift pot** (optional): funds explicitly earmarked for Immediate-mode use, no expectation of return

### Layer 4: Just-in-time conversion
- Convert from store pot to transaction pot only when needed
- For most people in stable-currency countries, this is just "transfer from savings to current"
- For dollarised-country people, this is "sell USDT to pesos at point of purchase"

### Layer 5: Account sweeping (optional advanced)
- End-of-day automation: any leftover transaction-pot funds sweep back to store pot
- Most banks and brokerages offer some version of this
- Removes the friction of remembering to do it manually

## What this beats

- **Anchoring** — you have a record, not a guess
- **Surveillance pricing** — you can detect when one merchant charges you more
- **Inflation surprise** — your personal index tells you in real time
- **Idle cash drag** — wealth always working
- **Mode confusion** — you've separated functions explicitly

## Why this is not "just being a sophisticated investor"

The framework's claim is not that everyone should become a financial professional. It is that the *architecture* (multi-pot, JIT conversion, personal index) is more important than the specific instruments. You can implement the stack with index funds, with a savings account, with crypto, with rotating savings clubs, with cattle — the *pattern* is what matters.

Doña Elena uses USDT and rental property. A subsistence farmer uses cattle and a small grain store. A Berlin office worker might use index funds and a high-yield savings account. The instruments differ; the architecture is the same.

## What this is NOT

- **Not a recommendation to use crypto** — crypto is one option among many for the store-pot
- **Not a recommendation to leave your bank** — most readers should not
- **Not a portfolio recommendation** — the framework is mode-neutral on which instruments to use
- **Not a get-rich strategy** — the goal is sight, not yield

## Why this matters

Defensive vocabulary becomes defensive practice. The reader who has built a personal stack:

- Cannot be inflated out of their savings without noticing
- Cannot be surveillance-priced without detecting it
- Cannot be locked into Obligated-mode subscriptions without knowing
- Has tools to refuse adversarial mode-forcing
- Has the same architecture Doña Elena uses to survive

The framework's promise: not to escape the bundle, but to *see* it and consciously route around it where you can.
